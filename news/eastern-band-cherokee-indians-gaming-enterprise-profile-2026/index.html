Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians: A Tribal Gaming Enterprise Profile

The Eastern Band built the Southeast's first major tribal gaming operation, then became the first tribe to own a commercial casino outside its own state.

By James KingfisherPublished September 10, 20266 min read

Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians gaming occupies an unusual position in Indian Country. The tribe operates two casino resorts in the mountains of western North Carolina under the Harrah's brand, has distributed gaming proceeds to its enrolled membership for decades, and in 2021 became the first tribal government in the United States to acquire and operate a commercial casino outside its own state. For a nation of roughly 16,000 enrolled members occupying the Qualla Boundary, that is an outsized footprint.

This profile sets out how the enterprise is structured, what it operates, and the pressures facing it as North Carolina's gambling market changes around it.

The properties

Harrah's Cherokee Casino Resort in Cherokee, North Carolina opened in 1997 and has been expanded repeatedly since. It is the tribe's flagship: a large gaming floor, multiple hotel towers, a substantial event center, and the food and beverage complement expected of a destination resort. Its location on the Qualla Boundary, adjacent to the Great Smoky Mountains National Park, gives it something few regional casinos have — a tourism base that would exist even without gaming.

Harrah's Cherokee Valley River Casino & Hotel opened in 2015 in Murphy, roughly two hours southwest, near the point where North Carolina, Georgia and Tennessee converge. Valley River was built to capture drive-in demand from north Georgia and eastern Tennessee that the Cherokee property could not efficiently reach, and it has since gone through its own expansion cycle, including the hotel tower work we covered in our report on the Valley River tower expansion.

Both properties operate under management agreements with Caesars Entertainment, which supplies the brand, the loyalty program integration and operational management. The arrangement is one of the longest-running tribal-commercial management relationships in the industry and predates the current generation of tribal enterprises that manage in-house. The regulatory framework governing such agreements is set out in our explainer on IGRA management contracts.

The Indiana acquisition

In 2021 EBCI Holdings, an entity of the tribe, acquired the operations of the Caesars-branded casino in Elizabeth, Indiana. The transaction made the Eastern Band the first tribe to own and run a commercial casino in another state — gaming conducted under an Indiana state license, not under IGRA, and therefore outside the tribal regulatory framework entirely.

The Indiana purchase reframed what a tribal gaming enterprise could be: not only an operator of gaming on its own lands, but an owner of licensed commercial assets anywhere a regulator will license it.

Several tribes have since followed variations of the same path, and the strategic logic has become a standing topic in tribal boardrooms. We examined the wider pattern in our analysis of tribal enterprises moving into commercial gaming beyond Indian lands. The appeal is diversification: revenue from a commercial asset is not exposed to compact renegotiation, exclusivity erosion or fee-to-trust litigation, though it is fully taxable and subject to a state regulator that owes the tribe no special deference.

Revenue use and the per capita question

The Eastern Band distributes a share of gaming net revenue to enrolled members through a revenue allocation plan, with minors' shares held in trust until adulthood and conditioned in part on educational attainment. The program is among the best-documented per capita distributions in Indian Country and is frequently cited in policy debates about the social effects of tribal gaming revenue.

The framework governing these distributions — which uses of net revenue IGRA permits, what a revenue allocation plan must contain, and how minors' trusts and tax treatment work — is explained in our pieces on IGRA's five permitted uses of net revenue and on minors' trusts and per capita taxation. The remaining share funds tribal government operations, health services, education, infrastructure and cultural programs on the Qualla Boundary.

Sports betting and the end of exclusivity

In March 2021 the tribe opened The Book at its two properties, bringing the first legal sports wagering to North Carolina under its Class III compact. For nearly three years the Eastern Band held effective exclusivity over legal sports betting in the state.

That ended when North Carolina authorized statewide mobile sports wagering, with online books launching in March 2024. The tribe retained a role — Caesars holds mobile rights on tribal lands, and the retail sportsbooks continue to operate — but the competitive position shifted fundamentally, from sole provider to one participant in an open commercial market. We reviewed the consequences in our two-year retrospective on North Carolina sports betting.

The episode is a useful case study for tribes elsewhere weighing whether to support state sports betting legislation. Exclusivity conferred by first-mover advantage rather than by compact language is exclusivity that can be legislated away. Our explainer on how tribal gaming exclusivity works sets out the distinction.

Where the enterprise stands

The Eastern Band enters the back half of the decade with a mature flagship property, a secondary property still growing into its market, a commercial asset in a different regulatory system, and a new competitive reality in its home state — including the arrival of a billion-dollar tribal resort near Charlotte that will sit between the Cherokee properties and their eastern feeder markets.

The strategic questions are the ones facing most mature tribal operators: how much further to push non-gaming amenity investment, whether to bring management in-house as the current agreements run their course, and how aggressively to pursue additional out-of-state commercial assets. None has an obvious answer. What the enterprise has demonstrated over nearly three decades is a willingness to move before the rest of the field does, which is how a tribe of 16,000 people came to be one of the more consequential gaming operators east of the Mississippi.
