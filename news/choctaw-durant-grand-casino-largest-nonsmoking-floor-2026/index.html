Choctaw Durant Unveils the Largest Non-Smoking Casino Floor

A six-phase renovation turns the Grand Casino into a flagship for the smoke-free wagering trend reshaping tribal gaming floors.

By Marcus WhitehorsePublished May 30, 20264 min read

The Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma has completed a phased overhaul of the Grand Casino inside Choctaw Casino & Resort in Durant, unveiling on May 13, 2026, what the operator describes as the largest non-smoking gaming floor in the United States. The project caps a renovation that began in January and rolled out across six phases, and it positions one of Oklahoma's marquee tribal properties at the front of a guest-comfort trend that has been quietly reshaping casino floors nationwide.

Upon completion, the Grand Casino floor houses roughly 5,400 slot machines and 83 table games, all in a smoke-free environment. The refresh introduced new carpeting, walkways and lighting for a more contemporary look, and games were shifted around the floor in stages so that play could continue while crews worked. To preserve guest choice, the resort's separate Link Casino continues to operate as a smoking area.

A staged renovation, not a shutdown

Rather than close the floor, Choctaw sequenced the work. The first phase opened on February 13 and immediately became non-smoking, and subsequent phases converted additional sections through the late spring. That approach let the property protect revenue during construction — an increasingly common playbook as large tribal operators reinvest gaming proceeds into their flagships without surrendering market share to competitors during the build.

Durant sits near the Texas border and draws heavily from the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, one of the largest feeder markets in American gaming. Keeping the floor open while modernizing it matters in a property whose customer base can easily choose among several destinations within a few hours' drive. The Choctaw Nation operates a network of casinos across southeastern Oklahoma, and the Durant resort is its largest; readers can find more context in our Choctaw Nation operator profile and on the Oklahoma state hub.

The smoke-free calculation

Non-smoking gaming has shifted from a niche accommodation to a competitive lever. Operators weigh a long-standing assumption that smokers gamble longer against mounting evidence that smoke-free environments broaden a property's appeal to younger guests, families and staff, while lowering ventilation and cleaning costs. By branding the Grand Casino around the size of its smoke-free floor, Choctaw is betting that comfort and air quality now sell, particularly to the suburban Texas visitors the property courts.

The move reflects a broader recalibration across Indian Country, where operators increasingly treat the gaming floor as a hospitality product to be designed rather than simply a room full of machines.

Choctaw is not acting in isolation. Smoke-free gaming has spread steadily since the pandemic prompted many properties to experiment with cleaner-air floors, and a number of tribal and commercial operators have since made non-smoking sections permanent fixtures rather than temporary accommodations. What distinguishes the Durant project is scale: by converting the entirety of its largest floor and marketing the result as a national record, Choctaw has turned a guest-comfort decision into a brand statement. The bet is that the visitors who once tolerated smoke will reward an operator that removes it, and that any play lost from dedicated smokers can be recaptured in the adjacent Link Casino, which remains a smoking environment.

The depth of Durant's feeder market gives the nation room to make that bet. With the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex a manageable drive away and no full-scale commercial casinos in Texas, Choctaw's southeastern Oklahoma properties function as the nearest destination gaming for millions of Texans. In that setting, amenities that widen a property's appeal — to non-smokers, to couples, to younger adults — translate directly into a larger addressable audience rather than merely a reshuffling of existing customers.

The decision also lands amid a wave of capital projects at tribal resorts. Across the country, operators are deploying record post-pandemic revenue into new towers, entertainment venues and floor redesigns, a trend documented in our 2025 tribal gaming economic impact report. The Durant refresh is comparatively modest next to ground-up tower expansions, but it signals the same underlying confidence: tribal operators reinvesting in the assets that already perform.

What it means for the market

For competitors, the unveiling raises the bar on amenity standards in a regional market that has grown steadily more sophisticated. A flagship floor that pairs a large non-smoking footprint with updated finishes pressures nearby properties to evaluate their own air-quality offerings, and it gives Choctaw a marketing message that is easy for casual visitors to understand.

For the Choctaw Nation, the value extends beyond the gaming floor. Casino revenue underwrites tribal government services — health care, education, housing and cultural programs — so a renovation that keeps the Durant property competitive is, in effect, an investment in the nation's broader economy. The smoke-free Grand Casino is unlikely to be the last word; phased reinvestment of this kind tends to continue as operators chase the next increment of guest experience. For now, Durant has staked a clear claim, and the rest of the regional market will be watching how the smoke-free flagship performs through its first full season.
