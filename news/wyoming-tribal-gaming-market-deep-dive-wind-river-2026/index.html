Wyoming Tribal Gaming: Inside the Wind River Reservation's Four Casinos

Wyoming has no commercial casinos. Its tribes still compete against two products the state authorised outside the compact framework.

By Daniel YazziePublished August 31, 20264 min read

Wyoming tribal gaming is the smallest state market in Indian Country that still functions as a genuine market, and it is unusual in almost every respect. All four of the state's tribal casinos sit on a single reservation. Two sovereign nations share that reservation and run competing properties on it. There are no commercial casinos in Wyoming to compete against, and yet the tribes face competition from two directions the compacting process never anticipated. For a market that will never appear near the top of a revenue table, Wind River is one of the more instructive case studies available.

Two nations, one reservation

The Wind River Indian Reservation in Fremont County is home to both the Northern Arapaho Tribe and the Eastern Shoshone Tribe — an arrangement created in the nineteenth century when the Northern Arapaho were settled on land reserved for the Eastern Shoshone. The two nations maintain separate governments, separate enrolments and, since the mid-2000s, separate gaming enterprises. The Northern Arapaho operate three facilities; the Eastern Shoshone operate one.

The Northern Arapaho flagship is Wind River Hotel & Casino in Riverton, the largest gaming property in the state. It offers more than 800 slot machines and roughly 20 live table games including blackjack, craps and roulette, along with a sportsbook lounge, a 90-room smoke-free hotel and multiple food outlets. The property also houses the Northern Arapaho Experience cultural room off the hotel lobby, where tribal artefacts, artwork and photography are displayed and elders are periodically present to share language and history — an integration of cultural interpretation into a gaming property that predates the current industry interest in the practice by more than a decade.

The tribe's two smaller properties serve local traffic. The Eastern Shoshone operate Shoshone Rose Casino & Hotel near Lander, a smaller full-service property with its own hotel component.

How the market was won

Wyoming's tribes did not receive Class III gaming; they litigated for it. The Northern Arapaho spent years pressing the state to negotiate a Class III compact under IGRA, and prevailed through the federal courts, with Wind River Hotel & Casino opening in 2005 as the result. That history is worth holding onto, because it makes Wyoming one of the clearer illustrations of what happens when a state declines to negotiate in good faith and a tribe has the resources and patience to pursue the statutory remedies. The path was long enough that it is not replicable for most tribes — which is precisely the point made in debates about the enforceability of IGRA's good-faith obligation after Seminole Tribe v. Florida. Our Legal Guide covers the compacting framework and its remedies.

The competitive picture is stranger than it looks

On paper, the Wind River tribes hold a monopoly: Wyoming has no commercial casinos. In practice they compete against two forms of gaming the state authorised outside the compact framework entirely.

The first is historical horse racing. Wyoming legalised pari-mutuel historical horse racing terminals in the 2010s, and HHR parlours have since proliferated across the state in bars, off-track betting locations and dedicated venues. The machines look and play very much like slot machines to a casual patron. For tribes whose compacts contemplate exclusivity over Class III device gaming, the spread of a functionally similar product under a racing statute is a direct erosion of the bargain — the same dispute now playing out in California, Kentucky and elsewhere. We examine the national picture in our analysis of HHR machines as a tribal exclusivity flashpoint.

The second is online sports wagering. Wyoming legalised statewide mobile sports betting in 2021 with an unusually open commercial framework and no requirement that operators partner with a land-based property. National operators serve the entire state from servers outside it. The Wind River properties operate retail sportsbook facilities and compete for the same handle without the customer acquisition budgets of the national books.

Wyoming granted its tribes Class III exclusivity in a state with no commercial casinos, then authorised two products that compete with Class III gaming under entirely different statutes.

Market fundamentals

Fremont County is large and sparsely populated. Riverton and Lander are the anchor communities, and the nearest substantial population centres — Casper to the east, the Front Range far to the south — are hours away. The properties therefore depend on a mix of genuinely local play, regional drive-in traffic from central Wyoming, and seasonal tourism moving along the US-26 and US-287 corridors toward Grand Teton and Yellowstone. That tourism component gives the market a pronounced summer peak that most tribal markets do not have, and a correspondingly thin winter.

The economic function of these enterprises is different from that of a destination resort. On a reservation where unemployment has historically run far above state and national averages, the casinos are among the largest non-governmental employers, and gaming revenue funds tribal government programmes in health, education and social services that would otherwise depend entirely on federal appropriations. Per-capita distribution decisions at Wind River have at times been contested within both tribes, a common tension covered in our explainer on revenue allocation plans.

What to watch

Three questions will shape the next several years. Whether either tribe pursues a compact amendment addressing HHR encroachment or online wagering, which would require a willing governor and a legislature that has shown little appetite for revisiting either statute. Whether the Northern Arapaho invest further in the Riverton property's non-gaming amenity set to capture more of the seasonal tourism corridor. And whether the two tribes coordinate at all on state-facing gaming policy — historically they have not, and a shared reservation with divided political representation is a weak position from which to negotiate with a state.
