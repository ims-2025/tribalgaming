Casino Del Sol Vahi Taa'am Opens as Tucson's First In-City Casino

The Pascua Yaqui Tribe's third Tucson-area property opened on land Congress placed in trust in 2022, adding a full Class III floor along Interstate 10.

By Sarah BegayPublished November 16, 20264 min read

The Pascua Yaqui Tribe opened Casino Del Sol, Vahi Taa'am to the public on Sunday, November 15, giving Tucson its first casino inside city limits and the tribe its third gaming property in the metropolitan area. The Casino Del Sol Vahi Taa'am opening followed media and VIP previews late last week and a morning ribbon-cutting on opening day, with a fireworks display scheduled for that evening, according to pre-opening announcements from the tribe and local coverage.

The property sits at 1055 W. Grant Road, beside Interstate 10, on land within the Old Pascua community, one of the tribe's historic settlements in the city. It joins Casino Del Sol Resort and Casino of the Sun, both on the Pascua Yaqui reservation southwest of Tucson, in the tribe's Casino Del Sol portfolio.

What opened on Grant Road

According to figures released before the opening and reported by KVOA, the building covers about 153,000 square feet, with a gaming floor of roughly 52,000 square feet. The floor carries 928 slot machines and live table games, with industry coverage putting the table count at 24. A sportsbook is part of the opening program, and the property includes multiple restaurants and bars, among them an upscale-casual venue called OP Grill. A four-level parking garage provides more than 1,500 spaces.

The tribe expects the property to support about 500 jobs across gaming, food and beverage, hospitality, security and administration. It has not publicly disclosed a total project cost, describing the casino instead as an investment in employment, tourism and economic development for the community.

The design is meant to be read from the freeway. A 26-foot copper dome is visible from Interstate 10, and inside, Tucson artist Joe Pagac painted a desert-sky scene across a roughly 24,000-square-foot domed ceiling. A central mural by Marcelino C. Flores III, titled "Aniam Yo'orine," or "Respect the World," anchors the floor. The name Vahi Taa'am comes from Yoeme, the Pascua Yaqui language, and tribal leaders have described it as a reference to three suns and a bright future. Our earlier report on the Yoeme name and design of the property covers the naming in more detail.

Amanda Sampson Lomayesva, interim chief executive of Casino Del Sol, described the casino as a statement of what the Pascua Yaqui Tribe stands for. Lesah Sesma, a 15-year veteran of the enterprise, is the property's general manager.

How the land became eligible for gaming

The opening caps a development path that ran through Congress rather than the standard administrative fee-to-trust process. The tribe bought the former Century Park 16 movie theater site about 15 years ago and began working to place it into federal trust in 2019. The decisive step was the Old Pascua Community Land Acquisition Act, Public Law 117-275, which President Biden signed on December 27, 2022. The bill was introduced by the late Rep. Raúl Grijalva of Arizona.

The statute directs the Interior Department to accept into trust, at the tribe's request, tribally owned land within a "Compact-Designated Area" bounded by West Grant Road, Interstate 10, West Calle Adelanto and North 15th Avenue. That area is defined by reference to the tribe's 2021 amended and restated gaming compact with Arizona. Land taken into trust under the Act becomes part of the reservation and is treated as if it had been acquired on September 18, 1978, and the law expressly makes gaming there subject to the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act. The effect was to resolve the post-1988 acquisition question that usually confronts new gaming sites. For how that mechanism works in general, see our Legal Guide to IGRA and Class III gaming.

Tucson also approved an intergovernmental agreement with the tribe covering the project. Demolition of the theater began in 2023, followed by roughly three years of construction.

A new competitive map for Tucson

Inside the Tucson metro, the new property changes the geography of tribal gaming. Until now, every casino serving the city sat on reservation land at its edges, including the Pascua Yaqui properties to the southwest and the Tohono O'odham Nation's Desert Diamond casinos. Vahi Taa'am places a full Class III floor and sportsbook along the city's main freeway corridor, positioned for the convenience visit that drives revenue at urban casinos. Our analysis of tribal casinos building inside city limits examines why that proximity matters.

The sportsbook operates within Arizona's dual regulatory structure, under which the state Department of Gaming and tribal regulators share oversight of tribal Class III gaming, including event wagering. Arizona's broader framework, which pairs tribal retail books with statewide mobile licenses, is described on our Arizona state hub.

Officials have also discussed later phases, with a convention center and resort hotel reportedly under consideration. No timeline or budget for those elements has been announced.

Vahi Taa'am is the last of three tribal casinos to open this fall, after Ho-Chunk Gaming Beloit in Wisconsin on October 23 and the North Fork Mono Casino in California's Madera County.

Taken together, the three openings show how differently tribes now arrive at a new gaming site: through a contested off-reservation two-part determination, through years of litigation, or, in the Pascua Yaqui case, through an act of Congress written around a compact-defined parcel. Our analysis of the fall 2026 openings sets out what each property will test over its first year. For Vahi Taa'am, the early measures will be visitation from central Tucson, how much play it draws from the tribe's existing properties, and how the sportsbook performs in a market where mobile wagering is already well established.
