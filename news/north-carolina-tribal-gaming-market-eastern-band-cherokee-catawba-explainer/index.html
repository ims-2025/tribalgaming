North Carolina Tribal Gaming Market: Cherokee, Catawba and What's Next

Two tribes hold the state's entire casino market. With Two Kings nearing completion and Georgia weighing casinos, the map is about to shift.

By Elena RuizPublished November 21, 20264 min read

North Carolina is one of the few states where tribal nations hold the entire legal casino market. There are no commercial casinos; the state's Class III gaming is conducted by two federally recognized tribes, the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians in the western mountains and the Catawba Nation near Charlotte. With the Catawba's permanent resort approaching its opening and statewide mobile sports betting now in its third year, the North Carolina tribal gaming market is entering a new phase. This explainer covers who operates, how the compacts work, and what is likely to change.

The Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians

The Eastern Band is descended from Cherokee people who remained in the Southern Appalachians after the removals of the 1830s, and its Qualla Boundary lands in western North Carolina are held in trust by the United States. The tribe signed its Class III compact with North Carolina in 1994 and opened Harrah's Cherokee Casino Resort in 1997, with Caesars Entertainment as manager. In 2015 it opened Harrah's Cherokee Valley River Casino & Hotel in Murphy, near the Georgia and Tennessee lines.

The compact has been expanded over time. Live-dealer table games arrived in 2012 after the General Assembly authorized them and the compact was amended, a change that turned the Cherokee resort into a full-scale regional destination drawing from the Southeast's large casino-less population. A 2019 state law allowed sports wagering at tribal casinos, and the Eastern Band opened retail sportsbooks in 2021. Our Eastern Band gaming enterprise profile covers the operation in depth.

The tribe has also diversified beyond its lands. In 2021 the Eastern Band, through its holding company, completed the purchase of Caesars Southern Indiana, an unusual instance of a tribal nation owning a commercial casino in another state. Revenue from gaming supports per capita distributions, health care, education and the tribal government's services.

The Catawba Nation

The Catawba Nation, headquartered in South Carolina, entered the North Carolina market through a long and contested process. In 2020 the Interior Department took land in Kings Mountain, North Carolina, into trust for the tribe, recognizing the site as within the Nation's historical service area. The decision drew a legal challenge from the Eastern Band, but the Catawba opened a temporary casino at the site in 2021 under a compact with the state.

The Nation has since been building its permanent Two Kings Casino Resort, a project reported at $1.25 billion, with an opening targeted for 2027. Its location on Interstate 85, roughly 35 miles west of Charlotte, gives it access to one of the fastest-growing metro areas in the country and to South Carolina, which has no casinos. Our opening checklist analysis tracks the remaining milestones.

North Carolina's market has grown from one tribe in the mountains to two tribes bracketing the state's western half, each drawing heavily from neighboring states without casinos.

The relationship between the two tribes is also worth understanding. The Eastern Band opposed the Catawba's Kings Mountain acquisition, arguing among other things that the site fell within Cherokee aboriginal territory. A federal court declined to block the project, and the temporary casino has operated since. The dispute illustrates a recurring pattern in Indian gaming: when a tribe seeks land away from its existing reservation, the most determined opposition sometimes comes from another tribe with an established market nearby. Sports betting and the mobile market

In 2023, North Carolina enacted legislation authorizing statewide mobile sports wagering, which launched in March 2024. Unlike Oklahoma or Florida, the state did not adopt a tribal-exclusive model; commercial operators can hold licenses, and the tribes participate alongside them. The Eastern Band's partnership with Caesars Sportsbook gave it a mobile presence. Our two-year retrospective looks at how the open-market approach has affected the tribes.

The same period saw a push in the General Assembly to authorize commercial casinos in rural "entertainment districts." The proposal was dropped in 2023 amid opposition, but it signaled that the tribes' de facto casino exclusivity is a matter of state policy, not a permanent guarantee.

What to watch

Three developments will shape the market. First is the Two Kings opening, which will substantially increase the Catawba's capacity and sharpen competition with the Eastern Band for Charlotte-area and Upstate South Carolina customers. Second is Georgia, where Governor-elect Keisha Lance Bottoms has called for a casino referendum; any Georgia market would affect Valley River and Cherokee, which draw heavily from north Georgia and metro Atlanta. Third is federal recognition for the Lumbee Tribe of North Carolina, the largest tribe east of the Mississippi River, which has long sought full federal recognition. Recognition would not automatically confer gaming rights, but it would raise questions about the state's tribal gaming map.
