San Manuel Buys Fairmont Scottsdale Princess in Tribal Hospitality Push

The Yuhaaviatam of San Manuel Nation's investment arm adds a 750-room Arizona resort to a portfolio spanning gaming and luxury hospitality.

By Elena RuizPublished October 14, 20264 min read

The San Manuel Investment Authority, the investment arm of the Yuhaaviatam of San Manuel Nation, has acquired the Fairmont Scottsdale Princess in Arizona, adding a 750-room luxury resort to a portfolio that already spans a California casino resort, a Las Vegas casino and a Southern California coastal hotel. The San Manuel Fairmont Scottsdale Princess acquisition, announced in early October 2026, is one of the clearer recent examples of a large tribal gaming enterprise placing capital in hospitality assets that sit well outside its gaming operations.

The seller was Strategic Hotels & Resorts. Financial terms were not officially disclosed, though trade reports citing Maricopa County recorder filings indicate that four parcels changed hands for roughly $241.4 million, a figure that reportedly includes close to $24 million in equipment, fixtures and other intangible assets. Because the tribe has not confirmed a price, the number is best read as a reported figure rather than an official one.

What the tribe bought

The Fairmont Scottsdale Princess occupies about 65 acres next to TPC Scottsdale, the course that hosts the WM Phoenix Open. It has 750 guest rooms and suites. According to the tribe's statement, the resort earned AAA's Timeless Diamond designation in 2025 and has held Five Diamond status since 1991. Fairmont will continue to operate the property, and local reporting quoted the resort's general manager saying the guest and community experiences, including its annual signature events, will continue.

San Manuel Chairwoman Lynn Valbuena said the tribe was pleased to add the resort to the luxury non-gaming properties it owns. Neither the announcement nor the early coverage described renovation plans, brand changes or a timeline for capital investment, and those questions remain open.

The buyer: an investment authority, not a casino operator

The purchaser is worth noting. The San Manuel Investment Authority is described in coverage as an unincorporated governmental instrumentality of the Nation. That structure is distinct from the tribe's gaming operation, which is best known through Yaamava' Resort & Casino at San Manuel in Highland, California. Separating the investment vehicle from the casino enterprise is a common governance choice among larger tribal operators, and it allows diversification decisions to be made, financed and reported on their own terms rather than inside the casino's operating budget. The broader legal framework for these entities is covered in our explainer on the structure of tribal gaming enterprises.

Taken together with earlier moves, the Scottsdale deal fills out a recognizable pattern:

In May 2021 the tribe bought the Palms Casino Resort in Las Vegas from Red Rock Resorts for $650 million in cash. In July 2023 it took a 40 percent stake in the Waldorf Astoria Monarch Beach in Dana Point, California, and by October 2025 it held majority control, with Hilton managing hotel operations and Troon managing golf. Scottsdale adds a third luxury resort to the non-casino side of the ledger. Nothing in the coverage suggests the Arizona property includes gaming, and Arizona's tribal gaming market, which we examine in our Arizona market deep dive, operates under compacts between tribes and the state rather than through commercial casino licenses.

Why diversification into hospitality

For a tribe whose flagship casino sits in one of the country's most competitive regional markets, buying luxury hotels in other states reduces dependence on a single property and a single state's compact. Hospitality real estate also brings different demand drivers than slot floors: group and leisure travel, golf and event business, and room revenue that does not track gaming volume. Our analysis of non-gaming revenue diversification looks at how widely this approach has spread, and a related piece on tribal enterprises operating beyond Indian lands covers the Las Vegas side of the pattern.

There is a reason this kind of acquisition draws attention in an industry that has historically reinvested in on-reservation expansion. Gaming revenue under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act must be used for specified governmental purposes, including funding tribal government operations and programs, promoting economic development and supporting the welfare of the tribe and its members. Investing in income-producing assets is generally understood as consistent with the economic development purpose, but each tribe's revenue allocation plan and ordinance governs how those decisions are made.

Open questions

Several details that observers will watch are not yet public. The first is the land and tax status of the Scottsdale parcels. Property that a tribe buys outside its reservation is typically held in fee and treated like other privately owned land for state and local tax purposes unless and until it is taken into trust, which is a separate federal process. The coverage reviewed here does not say how the property will be held or taxed, and we have not assumed an answer. Our explainer on land into trust lays out why the distinction matters.

The second is the operating model: whether Fairmont's management arrangement continues unchanged, and whether the investment authority pursues capital projects. The third is financing. Reported prices of this size are often supported by a mix of tribal equity and debt, but no financing details have been disclosed.

For the sector, the practical takeaway is straightforward. The largest tribal operators are increasingly behaving like diversified owner-operators, with gaming as the cash engine and hospitality real estate as a complementary holding. How well that strategy performs will depend on hotel market conditions in Scottsdale and on the discipline of the investment authority's governance, neither of which can be judged from a purchase announcement alone. We will follow the transaction as the tribe or the property releases more information.
