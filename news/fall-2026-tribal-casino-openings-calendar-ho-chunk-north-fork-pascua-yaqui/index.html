Fall 2026 Tribal Casino Openings: Ho-Chunk, North Fork, Pascua Yaqui

Wisconsin, California and Arizona each get a new tribal casino within a month, and the three projects sit in very different legal and financial positions.

By Marcus WhitehorsePublished October 21, 20264 min read

Three tribal casino projects in three states are scheduled to open within roughly three weeks of one another, a cluster that makes the fall of 2026 one of the busiest stretches on the tribal gaming calendar this year. Ho-Chunk Gaming Beloit is set to hold its grand opening on October 23, the North Fork Mono Casino near Madera, California, is scheduled to open at midnight on October 28, and Casino Del Sol Vahi Taa’am, the new Pascua Yaqui property in Tucson, has been counting down to a November 15 opening. Each project is different in scale, financing and regulatory history, but together they show where tribal casino capital is going and how much of the sector’s development risk is legal rather than physical.

Ho-Chunk Gaming Beloit: October 23

The Ho-Chunk Nation’s Beloit casino, near the Wisconsin–Illinois state line, moved its grand opening from September 26 to October 23, 2026. The first phase is planned to include roughly 1,300 slot machines, table games and live poker. A phased opening is common for large tribal projects: the gaming floor and core food service open first so that the property begins producing revenue, while hotel, event and ancillary components follow. We covered the schedule change and phase-one scope in our earlier report on the Beloit opening.

Beloit is notable because it sits in a market where Wisconsin tribes compete not only with each other but with Illinois commercial operators just across the line. For readers following the broader regional picture, our Wisconsin market deep dive explains how the state’s compacts and tribal land base shape that competition.

North Fork Mono Casino: October 28

The largest of the three is the North Fork Rancheria of Mono Indians’ casino off Highway 99 in Madera County. Reported specifications describe a 264,000-square-foot facility costing about $725 million, with more than 2,400 slot machines, about 40 table games, eight dining venues, four bars and more than 1,500 jobs. Station Casinos, the Las Vegas operator, runs the property under a management contract signed in 2024. The tribe’s financing arrangements are described in our coverage of the $725 million Madera financing.

What distinguishes North Fork from the other two projects is the length of its path. The land and compact history stretches back to a 2011 federal determination and a 2012 compact signed by then-Governor Jerry Brown, followed by a 2014 statewide referendum that rejected the compact. Interior issued secretarial procedures in 2016 that allowed the tribe to proceed. A state appeals court later ruled that the 2014 vote annulled the governor’s concurrence, and the California Supreme Court declined to review that decision. Separately, the Picayune Rancheria of Chukchansi Indians, which operates Chukchansi Gold Resort and Casino in Coarsegold, filed a federal suit on June 5 challenging the National Indian Gaming Commission’s approval of the Station management contract. A hearing on a preliminary injunction and related motions was held on October 9 before U.S. District Judge Kirk E. Sherriff, and at the time of the most recent reports no ruling had been issued. Our report on the Chukchansi lawsuit and the October opening tracks that case.

An opening date is a physical milestone, but for North Fork it is also a legal one: the casino is scheduled to open while a federal challenge to its management contract remains undecided.

Casino Del Sol Vahi Taa’am: November 15

The Pascua Yaqui Tribe’s new Tucson-area property is the third entry in the cluster. The tribe has been in the final countdown to a November 15 opening, and our profile of the project describes what the property adds to the tribe’s existing gaming operations. Arizona’s compact structure, including the sliding-scale contributions tribes make to the state, affects how the economics of a new Class III property are measured; our Arizona state hub collects the relevant background.

What the cluster says about the market

Several observations follow from the calendar itself. First, tribal operators continue to commit large amounts of capital to physical properties even as attention in the industry has shifted toward sports betting and online products. The three projects are, at their core, slot-and-table-game destinations. Second, the projects are in different regulatory lanes: Beloit operates under a Wisconsin compact, North Fork under secretarial procedures in California, and Pascua Yaqui under Arizona’s compact framework. A reader trying to compare them should start with the differences in IGRA and Class III authority rather than with the size of the buildings.

Third, the timing underscores how often opening dates move. Beloit’s slip of nearly a month is modest by industry standards, but it is a reminder that announced dates are targets until the doors open. Readers who want to follow the full development pipeline can consult our construction pipeline analysis, and the events calendar lists industry gatherings where operators typically discuss openings.

What to watch

For Beloit, the key questions are how quickly phase one ramps and when later phases are scheduled. For North Fork, observers will watch both the opening itself and any ruling on the Chukchansi motions, since a decision for or against the preliminary injunction would shape the casino’s first months. For Pascua Yaqui, the focus will be on how the new property fits alongside the tribe’s existing facilities. None of these outcomes is certain, and TribalGaming.com will report results as they are confirmed by the tribes and the courts.
