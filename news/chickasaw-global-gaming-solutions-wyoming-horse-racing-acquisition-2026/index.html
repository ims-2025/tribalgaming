Chickasaw Nation's Global Gaming Solutions Buys Wyoming Horse Racing

Global Gaming Solutions takes over a state-regulated racing and historical horse racing network, extending a tribal portfolio far beyond Oklahoma.

By Marcus WhitehorsePublished October 30, 20264 min read

The Chickasaw Nation has widened its commercial gaming footprint into Wyoming. Global Gaming Solutions, the nation's gaming business subsidiary, has acquired the operations of Wyoming Horse Racing, a state-regulated network of off-track betting parlors, historical horse racing machines and live racing. The Wyoming Gaming Commission approved the transfer, including live-event and simulcast permits, at a special meeting in mid-October, and the commission confirmed the approval to Cowboy State Daily on October 20. The Chickasaw Nation's Wyoming horse racing acquisition did not disclose a price.

The transaction is notable less for its size than for what it says about how large tribal enterprises now think about growth. Rather than relying only on gaming on trust land, an increasing number of well-capitalized tribal businesses are buying state-licensed assets where the legal framework is the state's, not the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act's.

What was acquired

According to reporting from Cowboy State Daily and Gaming Intelligence, the deal covers the entire operations of the Wyoming company, which trade reporting describes as about 13 Horse Palace locations, more than 1,500 historical horse racing machines, and live racing at Sweetwater Downs Racetrack in Rock Springs. Among the best-known sites is the Horse Palace Swan Ranch in Cheyenne, a roughly $25 million, 30,000-square-foot facility that opened in June 2024 along southbound Interstate 25 about four miles from the Colorado border. A Horse Palace in Evanston opened in February 2026.

The seller was not named in the coverage reviewed. Earlier reporting indicates that Pacesetter Racing and Gaming and a financing partner bought the business from its founder in December 2021, and a joint venture involving an affiliate of Sculptor Real Estate appears among the advisors on the sell side. Neither side disclosed terms. Citizens Capital Markets and Advisory and Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck advised Global Gaming Solutions, according to the reporting.

"Diversification of our business portfolio remains an integral business strategy," Chickasaw Gov. Chris Anoatubby said of the acquisition.

Who Global Gaming Solutions is

Global Gaming Solutions is the Chickasaw Nation's vehicle for gaming operations that sit outside the nation's own casino system. Its portfolio already includes Remington Park in Oklahoma City and Lone Star Park in Grand Prairie, Texas, both racetrack properties, and it manages close to 1,400 hotel rooms at WinStar World Casino and Resort. The company reports more than 1,250 direct employees across Oklahoma, Texas, California and Wyoming. Dan Boren chairs its board and Skip Seeley serves as president and chief executive.

That portfolio helps explain the Wyoming purchase. Racetracks and pari-mutuel networks are the kind of regulated, license-dependent business a tribal operator with in-house expertise in compliance, hospitality and slot-style content can run credibly. For a broader look at how the nation structures its business holdings, see our Chickasaw Nation enterprise profile.

Why the legal framework matters

The Wyoming facilities are not tribal gaming in the IGRA sense. Because they sit on state land rather than sovereign tribal land, they remain under state rules and state regulators, as the Wyoming Gaming Commission's role in approving the transfer illustrates. Wyoming law ties historical horse racing machines, which resemble slot machines but pay out from pools built on previously run races, to the existence of live racing. An operator must maintain a live track to keep the machines on the floor, a point a commission official made when describing why the machines were authorized.

That structure has two practical consequences. First, there is no compact to negotiate and no tribal-state revenue-sharing formula; the operator pays whatever the state's tax and fee structure requires. Second, the Chickasaw Nation takes on the same licensing exposure as any commercial operator, including permit renewals and the obligation to keep the racing product viable. Wyoming's tribal gaming landscape

With this deal, the Chickasaw Nation becomes the third tribe with a presence in Wyoming's gaming economy, according to Cowboy State Daily. The Northern Arapaho Tribe and the Eastern Shoshone Tribe operate casinos on the Wind River Indian Reservation near Riverton under federal rules. The Chickasaw presence is of a different kind: an outside tribal owner running state-regulated assets. We examine the market in more detail in our Wyoming tribal gaming market deep dive.

For Oklahoma readers, the acquisition fits a pattern visible across the state's larger tribal enterprises, which have spent years diversifying beyond home-market casinos. Our Oklahoma state hub tracks those operators.

What to watch

Several questions remain open. The purchase price and financing have not been disclosed. It is not yet clear whether Global Gaming Solutions will change the Horse Palace format, invest further in the Colorado-border Cheyenne market, or adjust the live racing calendar at Sweetwater Downs. The company has also said an agreement framework would bring the All-American Triple Crown Series to Lone Star Park in 2027, a sign that racing remains central to its strategy.

What the transaction does show is that tribal gaming capital increasingly flows wherever a defensible, regulated position can be bought, including places where the tribe's sovereignty plays no direct role. The terms of that trade-off, no exclusivity but also no compact, will matter as more tribal enterprises consider similar moves.
