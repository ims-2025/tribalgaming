Prairie Island's Backup Plan: Treasure Island and the Elk Run Option

With new leadership at Treasure Island, the Prairie Island Indian Community's federally approved fallback site is a rare case of land policy as risk management.

By Daniel YazziePublished November 27, 20264 min read

Few tribal casinos in the United States sit as close to a nuclear power plant as Treasure Island Resort & Casino, the Prairie Island Indian Community's flagship on the Mississippi River near Red Wing, Minnesota. The Prairie Island Nuclear Generating Plant and its spent-fuel storage area lie roughly 700 yards from the casino, according to Minnesota Star Tribune reporting. That proximity, along with the community's location in a river floodplain, has shaped one of the more unusual land strategies in tribal gaming: a federally approved option for an emergency casino site roughly an hour away, north of Rochester. With a new general manager appointed this year and a rebuilt hotel tower in service, Prairie Island's approach to keeping its gaming operation running is worth a closer look.

Treasure Island's position in the Minnesota market

Treasure Island is one of the largest tribal casinos in Minnesota and a major employer in Goodhue County. In August 2026, the Prairie Island Tribal Council appointed longtime executive Mike Heavner as general manager of the resort. The property's 246-room Buffalo Tower reopened after a year-long overhaul, part of a broader effort to keep the resort competitive with larger Twin Cities-area operators, including Mystic Lake, which is owned by the Shakopee Mdewakanton Sioux Community.

Minnesota's tribal casinos operate under compacts signed in 1989 and 1991 that authorize video games of chance and blackjack and have no expiration date and no revenue-sharing payments to the state. That framework, covered in our Minnesota state hub, gives the state's 11 tribal nations long-term certainty on gaming but little flexibility on new products. Sports betting, for example, would require state legislation, which is again expected to be debated in 2027, as we outlined in our analysis of Minnesota tribal sports betting prospects.

The Prairie Island emergency casino option

The Prairie Island Indian Community has spent decades living next to a facility it did not choose. The nuclear plant began operating in the 1970s, and federal delays in building a permanent national repository for spent fuel left the waste stored on-site in dry casks near tribal homes. The community has long pursued land away from the island for housing and future development.

In 2019 the community bought roughly 1,200 acres known as Elk Run, along U.S. Highway 52 north of Rochester, for $15.5 million. The site had been planned as a biotech park that never materialized. In December 2024, the Bureau of Indian Affairs took about 400 of those acres into federal trust. As reported by the Star Tribune, the tribe's application described a possible "emergency gaming facility" that could open in a vacant 22,000-square-foot barn on the property if a flood or a nuclear incident forced Treasure Island to close. The decision also contemplates a permanent casino at the site after a six-year forbearance period. The remaining 800 acres, closer to Pine Island, were the subject of a separate request focused on member housing.

The tribe said at the time it had no immediate plans to develop gaming at Elk Run. The significance lies less in any near-term construction than in the fact that the option exists. Gaming on land acquired after IGRA's 1988 enactment is generally prohibited unless it fits one of the statute's exceptions or receives a two-part Secretarial determination with the governor's concurrence. That process is discussed in our analysis of the Little Shell two-part determination. For a tribe whose only casino sits next to a nuclear plant on a floodplain, securing a federally recognized path to a second gaming site is a form of insurance that money alone cannot buy.

Most casino business-continuity plans cover backup power, cash reserves and cyber recovery. Prairie Island's plan covers the possibility that the entire site becomes unusable.

Business continuity as a sovereignty issue

Tribal gaming operators have become more attentive to continuity risk in recent years. Pandemic closures in 2020 showed how quickly a single-property tribe could lose most of its government revenue. Cyberattacks have since forced several casinos to operate on cash-only systems for days or weeks, an issue examined in our coverage of cyber-incident fallback planning. Rising property insurance premiums, especially for properties exposed to flood or wildfire, have added financial pressure.

For most tribes, continuity planning means reserve funds, insurance and redundant systems. Prairie Island's situation shows that for some, it also means land. A tribal government that depends on gaming revenue to fund health care, education and housing cannot easily absorb a closure measured in years rather than weeks. Having trust land already designated for a possible emergency facility shortens what would otherwise be a lengthy federal approval process at the worst possible moment.

The approach also has limits. An emergency facility in a converted barn would be a stopgap, not a replacement for a resort with hundreds of hotel rooms. Any gaming at Elk Run would also need to fit within Minnesota's compact structure and federal requirements for facility licensing and regulation. The views of local governments near the site, which saw the earlier biotech plans for Elk Run collapse, would also matter.

What comes next

For now, Treasure Island remains the community's sole gaming operation, and its new leadership is focused on the resort itself: the renovated tower, the gaming floor and competition with metro-area casinos. The Elk Run option stays in reserve. If the federal government eventually moves spent fuel away from Prairie Island, the contingency may never be needed. Until then, it is one of the clearest examples of a tribe treating land policy as part of gaming risk management. Compare Minnesota properties and amenities on our comparison page.
