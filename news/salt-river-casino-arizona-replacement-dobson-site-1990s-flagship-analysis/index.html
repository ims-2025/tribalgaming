Salt River Weighs a Casino Arizona Replacement as 1990s Flagships Age

About 80 acres of a 270-acre redevelopment site are reserved for gaming as the Community weighs whether to rebuild rather than renovate.

By Daniel YazziePublished November 18, 20264 min read

The Salt River Pima-Maricopa Indian Community is evaluating a new casino to replace Casino Arizona, the property its gaming enterprise opened in 1998, and has reserved roughly 80 acres of a large redevelopment site for a possible gaming facility. A Casino Arizona replacement would be one of the more significant capital decisions in the Phoenix-area tribal market this decade, and it reflects a broader question facing first-generation tribal casinos across the country: when a building that was designed for the gaming market of the 1990s reaches its limits, is it better to keep renovating or to start over?

The Community has not announced a final plan, a budget or a construction timeline, and Casino Arizona remains open. But the planning record published to date is detailed enough to show how the decision is being framed.

What the Community has disclosed

Tribal reporting in October 2025 said the gaming floor at Casino Arizona had reached full capacity after numerous renovations. The Community's gaming enterprise has said it is evaluating a new casino that would be, in its words, "completely owned and operated by the Community." The 2021 amended Arizona compact, which expanded the games and machine capacity available to tribes statewide, was cited as a reason new opportunities had opened.

The candidate site is the former Salt River Materials Group Dobson property, about 270 acres of Community-owned land along the McKellips Road corridor near Mesa. On November 3, 2025, the Community issued a request for interest seeking non-casino development proposals for the site, with responses due January 30, 2026. A later addendum said a request for proposals would follow, with timing subject to change. As of late September, the Community's redevelopment webpage said only that updates were coming soon.

The preliminary layout shows how gaming and commercial uses would fit together. A proposed extension of Dobson Road would divide the property. About 147 acres west of the road would be available for commercial development. East of the road, at least 38 acres are designated for commercial use and about 80 acres are reserved for an adjacent gaming facility. The Community is working with the Maricopa County Department of Transportation on extending Dobson Road north from Mesa across the Salt River, which would materially change access to the site.

Why 1990s flagships are being rethought

Casino Arizona belongs to a generation of tribal casinos built in the years immediately after the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act and the first wave of tribal-state compacts. Many of those buildings were designed around slot rows, a modest number of table games, buffets and surface parking. They were often expanded in stages as demand grew, with additions attached to the original structure.

Three changes have strained that model. The first is the game mix. Arizona's 2021 compact amendments allowed tribes to add craps, roulette, baccarat, pai gow and sic bo, and the accompanying state law authorized event wagering. Table pits and sportsbook lounges require different floor space, sightlines and surveillance coverage than the original layouts anticipated. Our Arizona tribal gaming market deep dive traces how those changes have reshaped properties around Phoenix and Tucson.

The second is the customer. Destination-style properties compete on dining, entertainment, hotel rooms and non-smoking space, all of which are harder to retrofit into an older box than to design into a new one. The Community already operates Talking Stick Resort, a full resort property, so it has direct experience with both formats.

The third is back-of-house infrastructure. Cage operations, count rooms, surveillance systems, kitchens and loading docks in older buildings were sized for smaller volumes. Renovation can modernize them, but often only by taking revenue-generating space offline while the casino stays open.

A replacement casino lets an operator design for the market it has now, open the new building and then close the old one, rather than renovating around live operations for years.

Replacement versus renovation

Other tribes in Arizona and elsewhere have faced the same choice. The Yavapai-Prescott Indian Tribe has pursued a consolidated casino project to bring operations into a single modern facility, and expansions across Oklahoma and California show the opposite approach of building onto existing sites. The right answer depends on land, access and the condition of the existing structure.

Salt River's situation has advantages that many tribes do not. The Dobson site is land the Community already owns, which means a replacement would not require the kind of off-reservation fee-to-trust process that has slowed projects elsewhere, provided the parcel sits within the Community's existing land base as its planning materials indicate. The site is also large enough to pair a casino with commercial development that could draw daytime traffic and generate non-gaming revenue for the Community.

There are also costs. A new building would require significant capital at a time when construction costs remain elevated, and the Community would need to manage the transition so that Casino Arizona's customers move with the operation. The Dobson Road extension, which is outside the Community's sole control, may also influence timing.

What to watch

The first signal will be the request for proposals for the commercial portions of the Dobson site, which would indicate how the Community intends to sequence development. A second will be any formal announcement on the gaming parcel itself, including size, program and budget. A third will be how a new facility fits into Arizona's compact framework, including the state contributions that tribes make under the compact; our analysis of Arizona tribal gaming contributions for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 shows the current trend in those payments.

For a casino that has operated for nearly three decades, the decision will set the Community's gaming footprint for the next one. The Arizona state hub tracks Salt River's properties alongside the state's other tribal operators.
