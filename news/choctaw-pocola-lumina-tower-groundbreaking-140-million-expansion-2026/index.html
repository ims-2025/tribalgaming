Choctaw Nation Breaks Ground on $140M Pocola Lumina Tower Expansion

A seven-story tower, a renovated casino floor and a restaurant reshuffle starting this month aim to turn a border gaming hall into a full-service resort.

By Daniel YazziePublished November 16, 20264 min read

The Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma broke ground on November 5 on the $140 million expansion of Choctaw Casino & Resort – Pocola, starting construction on a project that will more than double hotel capacity at the tribe's property on the Arkansas border. The Choctaw Pocola expansion centers on a new seven-story hotel tower, named the Lumina Tower, along with a full renovation of the existing hotel, casino floor and food and beverage spaces. Chief Gary Batton and Assistant Chief Jack Austin joined Tribal Council members at the ceremony, according to local coverage of the event.

The tribe first announced the project in June, when it said a groundbreaking would follow in the fall. Our earlier report on the announcement covered the initial plan. The ceremony now puts a construction schedule and a builder behind it.

What the expansion includes

The Lumina Tower will add 130 guest rooms, including premium suites, to the property's existing 118-room Vista Tower. New amenities include a resort-style pool with private cabanas and an outdoor bar, a full-service spa, a 24-hour fitness center and a new coffee and tea bar. The Vista Tower rooms will be renovated with new carpet, lighting, bathroom tile, fixtures and furnishings, and the lobby and guest registration area will be refreshed.

On the casino side, the plan calls for new carpet and lighting on the gaming floor and remodeled restrooms. The poker room, off-track betting area and rewards club will move to upgraded locations. Restaurants and bars will be refreshed with new branding and expanded food and cocktail offerings.

The tribe has set a 28-month construction timeline, with Tutor Perini Construction and JCJ Architecture as partners. The project is expected to create more than 150 new jobs and generate an estimated $20 million in economic impact, according to figures released at the groundbreaking.

Some changes will reach guests well before the tower opens. Interior restaurant renovations are scheduled to begin November 29. The Square Pie outlet will close, Trophy's Bar & Grill will move into its space, and Trophy's current location will become IronWood Table, a casual dining restaurant that will be the first new venue to open under the plan.

General manager Christy Chaser described the goal as transforming the property into a full-service resort.

Why Pocola matters to the Choctaw portfolio

Pocola began as a bingo hall in 1994. It now offers a hotel, a large gaming floor, several restaurants and more than 7,000 square feet of meeting space. Chief Batton has described it as the largest employer in LeFlore County and the second-largest resort in the Choctaw Casinos & Resorts portfolio, behind the flagship in Durant.

The location explains the investment. Pocola sits minutes from Fort Smith, Arkansas, and draws much of its business from across the state line. Arkansas now has commercial casinos, but none in the Fort Smith area, which leaves Pocola as the closest full casino for much of western Arkansas. A larger hotel with resort amenities is designed to turn more day visits from that market into overnight stays, and to let the property compete for events and group business that it has historically ceded to larger resorts. Our Oklahoma state hub maps the tribal properties along the state's borders.

The expansion also fits a broader pattern at Choctaw Nation properties. The tribe completed a non-smoking transition at the Grand Casino in Durant earlier this year, and it has extended its hospitality business through a hotel brand arrangement with Wyndham. Pocola's renovation follows the same logic of upgrading regional properties so that gaming revenue is supported by lodging, dining and amenities.

The Oklahoma backdrop

The project proceeds under Oklahoma's model gaming compact, under which tribes pay the state exclusivity fees on covered games in return for exclusive rights to those games. Those payments fund state programs, including education, and the system has generated steady revenue for both tribes and the state since the compacts took effect in 2005. Our analysis of Oklahoma exclusivity fees explains where that money goes.

Large hotel and amenity spending is a long-term bet, and it reflects confidence in that framework. The Pocola tower is scheduled for completion in early 2029 under the stated timeline. That puts its opening after the start of the next governor's term, a period in which sports betting and compact relations are expected to return to the agenda in Oklahoma City.

Seen in that light, Pocola is a modest project by the standards of the largest tribal resorts, but a significant one for a single-county market, where 130 new rooms and a spa change what the property can sell on a weekend. For now the work is physical: a seven-story tower going up beside an operating casino, and a restaurant reshuffle starting at the end of the month. As at other tribal resorts that renovate while open, the operating challenge will be keeping the gaming floor productive while construction moves around it. The Choctaw Nation has not announced interim changes to casino hours or gaming operations during the build.
