New Governors, Old Compacts: What the 2026 Class Means for Tribal Gaming

From Mazzei in Oklahoma to Hinojosa in Texas, the people who will sign or stall compact amendments in 2027 are changing in key states.

By Elena RuizPublished November 13, 20264 min read

The November 3 elections produced a large class of new governors, and in several states where tribal gaming is a live policy issue, the person who will sign or refuse compact amendments in 2027 is someone new. For tribes, the governors-elect and tribal gaming compacts question is concrete: governors negotiate Class III compacts under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act, and in most states no amendment moves without them.

Before the election, we looked at how term-limited governors created a window of uncertainty for pending compacts. With results now called in most races, it is possible to look at the specific states where leadership is changing and what the record suggests about each incoming governor. This analysis relies on reported results and on what the governors-elect have said publicly. Where they have not stated positions on gaming, we say so.

Oklahoma: a reset after the Stitt years

Republican Mike Mazzei won the Oklahoma race and takes office in January, succeeding Gov. Kevin Stitt. Few states have seen a more strained governor-tribe relationship in recent years. Stitt's 2019 push to renegotiate the state's model gaming compacts led to litigation, and his later separate compacts with individual tribes drew legal challenges. His relationship with tribal leaders broke down further in 2023 over sports betting, and the legislature overrode several of his vetoes on tribal priorities.

Tribal leaders responded to Mazzei's win with cautious optimism. Muscogee Nation Principal Chief David Hill said, "Let's just start off with a clean slate and see what we can do in the next four years," according to KOSU. Cherokee Nation Principal Chief Chuck Hoskin Jr. said the nation was ready to work with the governor-elect. Mazzei said his job would be to be "a tough but fair negotiator" for Oklahomans.

On sports betting, Mazzei did not make it a campaign issue. Reporting from The Lines indicates he would not block a bill the tribes support. The legislative math remains the harder variable, as we detailed in our look at Oklahoma Senate turnover and the 2027 outlook. The Oklahoma state hub profiles the state's major gaming tribes.

California, Florida and Texas: three large markets, three different starting points

In California, Democrat Xavier Becerra defeated Republican Steve Hilton and will succeed Gov. Gavin Newsom. Several tribal governments, including the Pechanga Band of Indians, Lytton Rancheria, and Yocha Dehe Wintun Nation, gave the maximum contribution to his campaign, according to reporting cited by CDC Gaming. As attorney general, Becerra sided with tribes in their disputes with commercial cardrooms. He has not said whether he supports gambling expansion. Newsom signed a number of compacts and amendments in 2026; Becerra will inherit any that remain in negotiation, along with whatever posture the state takes toward the tribes' planned 2028 sports betting measure.

In Florida, Republican Byron Donalds succeeds Gov. Ron DeSantis, who signed the 2021 compact with the Seminole Tribe of Florida that authorized the tribe's sports wagering hub. The tribe gave $1 million to the Friends of Byron Donalds political committee this year, according to Florida Politics, continuing a pattern of support it extended to DeSantis. The compact runs for decades, so Donalds is not facing a renegotiation; his role will be in how the state administers the existing agreement and responds to any expansion proposals. Our Seminole compact analysis covers the agreement's structure.

Texas produced the most surprising result. Democrat Gina Hinojosa defeated Gov. Greg Abbott. Texas's three federally recognized tribes have no Class III compacts, and casino gaming requires a constitutional amendment. Proposals such as HJR 52 would put the question to voters and open compact talks with the tribes. A governor's support does not change the requirement for two-thirds legislative approval of a constitutional amendment, but it changes who would be at the table if one passed.

A governor cannot change IGRA, but in most states a governor decides whether compact talks happen at all, and how quickly.

Wisconsin, Minnesota, Michigan and New Mexico

In Wisconsin, Democrat David Crowley succeeds Gov. Tony Evers, whose administration negotiated compact amendments with the state's tribes. Wisconsin's tribal online sports betting framework is currently being defended in court by eight intervening tribes, and any further amendments implementing it would cross the new governor's desk.

In Minnesota, Democrat Amy Klobuchar won the governorship. Minnesota's tribal sports betting bill has stalled repeatedly in the legislature over the tribes-versus-racetracks dispute; a governor's signature has not been the bottleneck there, but executive engagement can shape the legislative compromise.

In Michigan, Democrat Jocelyn Benson succeeds Gov. Gretchen Whitmer. Michigan's tribes have been in a dispute with the state over compact payments tied to exclusivity, a matter that will now be handled by a new administration.

In New Mexico, Democrat Deb Haaland, a citizen of the Pueblo of Laguna and former Secretary of the Interior, won the governorship. She will be the first Native American woman elected governor of a U.S. state. Her Interior tenure included oversight of the department's Office of Indian Gaming, which reviews compacts nationwide.

Where governors are staying put

Continuity matters too. Gov. Kathy Hochul won re-election in New York, where the Seneca Nation's compact talks face a December deadline. Gov. Katie Hobbs won re-election in Arizona, and Gov. Ned Lamont in Connecticut. In those states, tribes negotiate with an administration they already know.

Most new governors take office in January. Compact amendments in progress before then may either be signed by outgoing administrations or held for successors. For tribes, the next several weeks will show which incoming teams name gaming advisers early and which treat the issue as a later-session matter.
