Fancy Dance Casino Expansion: Ponca Tribe Grows Perry, Oklahoma Floor

A $20 million-plus project would take the Perry property from roughly 290 electronic games to more than 500 and add a sports bar, new dining and upgraded technology.

By Marcus WhitehorsePublished October 12, 20265 min read

The Fancy Dance Casino expansion in Perry, Oklahoma, is a more-than-$20 million project that will roughly double the gaming floor of a property the Ponca Tribe of Indians of Oklahoma opened in September 2020. According to a report published September 23, 2026, the project will lift the casino from more than 290 electronic games on about 10,000 square feet to more than 500 games, and is scheduled to open in late 2026.

The first phase began in November 2025. Under the property's master plan, that phase covered the expanded casino area, a new cage, restaurant and bar space, back-of-house facilities and parking. The tribe is also adding a sports bar called Buffalo Post 38, which will offer food, a full-service bar and space to watch sporting events, along with other new dining and entertainment offerings.

What the Fancy Dance Casino expansion includes

The numbers describe a significant change in scale. Going from a little over 290 machines to more than 500 is an increase of more than 200 games, which at this size is a rebuild of the product rather than a refresh. Floor capacity is the most direct lever a regional casino has: it determines how many guests can play at peak hours, how much of the local market the property can serve without turning people away, and how much floor-level variety the operator can offer a loyal base.

The food and beverage side matters as much as the floor. Buffalo Post 38 adds a destination built around live sports viewing, the kind of amenity that extends the length of a visit and gives a property a reason to be on a guest's calendar beyond slot play. The report also notes a technology upgrade: a PDQ point-of-sale platform from Signature Systems for food-and-beverage operations and loyalty functions. Linking point-of-sale data to loyalty accounts is a routine step for operators that want to understand total guest spend, not just gaming play, and it is the kind of back-end investment that rarely gets attention but shapes how a property is run.

Several details were not available in the public reporting at the time of writing, including the exact opening date within late 2026, the number of jobs the project is expected to create, and the financing structure. We will not speculate on them here. What is on the record is the cost floor of more than $20 million, the game counts before and after, and the scope of the first phase.

Why a mid-size tribal casino expansion matters in Oklahoma

Oklahoma is among the most developed tribal gaming markets in the country, and that context shapes every expansion decision. Operators there compete on convenience and product quality rather than scarcity, and a property in a smaller community such as Perry has to give regional customers a reason to choose it over a larger resort a short drive away. Expansion is one of the few tools available: more games, better food, and a more complete evening out. Our analysis of Oklahoma market maturity examines how that competitive dynamic plays out across the state, and the Fancy Dance project fits the pattern of reinvestment in an established market rather than entry into a new one.

The timing is also notable. The casino opened in 2020, so the expansion arrives roughly six years after opening, with the first phase starting about five years in. For a young property, that is a typical window in which an operator has enough operating history to judge demand and decide where added capacity will pay off. A tribe that builds a casino and then returns to expand it is making a judgment that the original floor is constrained, not that the market is weak.

For the Ponca Tribe of Indians of Oklahoma, the project also reflects the broader role gaming plays in tribal government finance. Under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act, net gaming revenue is directed to tribal government operations, services, general welfare, economic development and charitable purposes, among other permitted uses. The Legal Guide explains how those permitted uses are structured and how Class III operations in states such as Oklahoma proceed under tribal-state compacts. A larger gaming operation generally means a larger base from which a tribe can fund its programs, although the tribe has not published projections tied to this project, and we do not offer any.

What to watch as the Perry project opens

Three questions will indicate how the project performs once it opens. The first is how quickly the new machines are placed and whether the opening coincides with the sports bar, since the report describes the expansion as a package rather than a staggered rollout. The second is staffing: a floor that grows by more than 200 games and adds a bar and dining space requires additional employees across gaming, food service and facilities, and labor availability has been a recurring constraint across the industry. The third is how the tribe markets the property to its existing base and to nearby communities, given that loyalty technology is part of the upgrade.

Readers tracking Oklahoma more broadly can find property listings and operator information on the Oklahoma state hub. We will update this story when the tribe confirms an opening date or releases further details about the final phase of construction.

The expansion of Fancy Dance Casino is a measured, capital-intensive bet by a tribal government on an existing asset. It is not a headline-grabbing megaproject, and it does not need to be: across Indian Country, much of the real growth in gaming comes from incremental investments like this one, made property by property, by tribes that know their local markets. The figures that are public — more than $20 million, more than 290 games becoming more than 500, a late-2026 target — are enough to show a tribe committing real capital to a six-year-old casino and betting that its guests want more of what it already offers.
