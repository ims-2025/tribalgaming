River Cree Resort Expansion: Enoch Cree Nation's $200M Tower

Exterior work is complete on the largest build in the resort's history, financed entirely by casino-generated revenue under full Enoch Cree ownership.

By Linda CardinalPublished October 26, 20264 min read

The Enoch Cree Nation has finished the exterior of the largest expansion in the history of River Cree Resort and Casino, a $200 million project that will roughly double the size of the property west of Edmonton. The River Cree Resort expansion, which the nation says is funded entirely from casino-generated revenue, is on track to open by July 2027, according to the nation.

The milestone was reported in June 2026, when the resort marked the end of its exterior renovations. Interior construction is now underway. Groundbreaking took place on September 7, 2024, making the build a roughly three-year undertaking from first shovel to opening.

What the $200 million buys

Reported components of the expansion include approximately 240 new hotel rooms in a 20-storey tower, whose top has been completed, along with about 40,000 square feet of event space, a new swimming pool, and additional dining options. A member of the nation's council has also referred to future additions, including a spa, though those are not part of the committed scope described publicly.

The scale is notable for Alberta's First Nations gaming sector. River Cree is a major employer and entertainment destination serving the Edmonton region, and the nation has framed the expansion as part of a broader ambition to make the site a self-contained "10-minute city," a place where visitors and residents can reach lodging, dining, entertainment, and services within a short distance. That language reflects a strategy of treating the property as a district rather than a single casino building.

Full ownership and the financing choice

River Cree's funding approach is as significant as its design. Enoch Cree Nation has been the sole owner of the resort since it bought out Paragon Gaming in 2014, and it later took over hotel management from Marriott. Funding a $200 million build from operating revenue, rather than relying on outside capital, is possible only for an operator with a strong cash position and a long-standing ownership structure. It also means the nation retains the full economic return and the decisions about timing, scope, and phasing. The ownership history is also a reminder that Canadian First Nations gaming has evolved through several models: provincially run facilities with revenue sharing, First Nation-owned and operated resorts, and partnerships with experienced management firms. Our explainer on U.S. tribal versus Canadian First Nations gaming legal models lays out how those arrangements differ from the U.S. framework under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act.

Alberta market context

River Cree's expansion arrives during a period of change in Alberta. The province's online gaming market opened in 2026, and First Nations have been working to position themselves within it, including through River Cree's own digital venture, River Cree iGaming Limited Partnership, which lists Indigenous Gaming Partners Inc. as general partner. We have covered that effort in River Cree's Alberta iGaming launch, and the revenue dimension in our look at the First Nations Development Fund and slot revenue grants.

A land-based expansion of this size during an online market launch raises a strategic question: whether in-person destination investment can hold its value as digital channels grow. Our analysis of iGaming cannibalization of land-based First Nations casinos weighs that debate. River Cree's answer appears to be that hotel rooms, events, and dining are not easily replicated online, and that a broader resort base reduces dependence on any single revenue line.

The same logic is visible farther east. Saskatchewan's First Nations-owned operator has also been expanding its footprint, a story we followed in our coverage of the SIGA expansion, and across the Prairies, nations are weighing how much to invest in physical assets versus digital ventures.

What to watch through 2027

Several factors will determine how the project performs once it opens. The first is the ramp-up of 240 additional rooms into a market that already has competing hotel supply. The second is the programming of the new event space, which will need a steady calendar to justify its size. The third is staffing: a larger resort requires more employees across hospitality, food service, and gaming, and labor availability remains a pressure point across the industry, as discussed in our review of construction cost inflation and the labor squeeze.

Public figures on projected revenue, employment gains, or return on investment have not been released in the sources reviewed for this article, and we have not attempted to estimate them. What the project does show is a nation with full ownership and a strong enough balance sheet to commit $200 million from its own resources, a position few operators in North America can claim. If the July 2027 timeline holds, River Cree will open the new tower roughly three years after groundbreaking, a pace consistent with large resort construction elsewhere on the continent.
