Kalispel Tribe Renames Cusick Casino Qlispé, Restoring Salish Spelling

A $10 million rural casino that now employs 60 tribal members will carry the name taught in the tribe's immersion classrooms.

By Marcus WhitehorsePublished November 13, 20264 min read

The Kalispel Tribe of Indians will rename its smaller casino in Cusick, Washington, in December. The Kalispel Casino becomes the Qlispé Casino, using the traditional Salish spelling of the tribe's name. The Kalispel casino rebrand, first reported by The Spokesman-Review on November 12, is a modest change in signage. It also reflects a broader pattern in tribal gaming: properties that once carried English-language or anglicized names are increasingly being branded in the tribe's own language.

Qlispé is pronounced kuh-lee-SPEH and translates roughly to "Camas People," a reference to the camas plant that the tribe relied on for food for generations. Tribal historian Annette Pierre told the newspaper that outsiders have spelled the tribe's name dozens of ways over the years, including "Calispelm," "Kullyspel," and "Coopspellar." JR Bluff, a tribal elder and executive director of language and culture, put it plainly: "There's nothing wrong with Qlispé. That's who we are."

A second casino that found its footing

The Cusick property is the Kalispel Tribe's second casino, sitting on the tribe's reservation in Pend Oreille County in northeast Washington. Curt Holmes, the tribe's vice chairman and its casino and government affairs director, pursued the project in 2019. The roughly $10 million development combined a casino with an RV park and a gas station. According to the Spokesman-Review, the tribe's initial hope was simply to break even.

Seven years later, Holmes said, the casino is profitable and employs 60 tribal members. It has also become a gathering place for tribal members on the reservation, a role that is easy to overlook in revenue figures but central to why many tribes build small, rural properties at all. The tribe's gaming income comes overwhelmingly from Northern Quest Resort & Casino in Airway Heights, west of Spokane, one of the largest tribal gaming properties in the Inland Northwest. The Cusick casino is a much smaller operation serving a rural market. The two together form a familiar tribal portfolio: a large metro-adjacent resort that funds government, and a small reservation-based property that anchors local employment. Gaming revenue and Salish language revival

The rebrand connects directly to how the Kalispel Tribe spends its gaming revenue. Holmes described the casinos' role bluntly: "This is our form of tax." Like other tribal governments, the Kalispel Tribe cannot rely on a property-tax or income-tax base on its reservation in the way states and counties do, so gaming revenue funds core government services.

According to the Spokesman-Review, that revenue supports police, medical, and dental services; scholarships for tribal members who return home with new expertise; and the Camas Center for Community Wellness, opened in 2008 with pools, a gym, a climbing wall, medical care, and dining. It also supports language revitalization. About 40 adult teachers learned conversational Salish through the tribe's programs and now teach more than 100 children in immersive classrooms, including a preschool.

A casino that once carried an anglicized spelling will now carry the name taught in the tribe's immersion classrooms, which gaming revenue helped build.

That loop, gaming revenue funding language programs that then reshape the gaming brand, is the substance of the change. Bluff said he hopes more children will learn the language early, and noted that nearby tribes are working on their own Salish dialects. Holmes said the rebrand will likely extend to other tribal properties over time.

Part of a wider naming shift

The Kalispel decision fits a pattern seen across Indian Country in recent years. In Tucson, the Pascua Yaqui Tribe chose a Yoeme name for its new property, Casino Del Sol Vahi Taa'am, a choice we examined in our report on the Vahi Taa'am name and design. Other tribes have folded language and art into resort design, signage, and room naming.

There are practical considerations for any rebrand. A new name has to be learned by customers, which is why operators often pair a change with pronunciation guides and local marketing. Signage, player-club materials, websites, and listings all need updating, and a regulator-facing record such as a facility license typically reflects the operating name. For a rural property whose customers are largely regional and local, those costs are manageable, and familiarity with the tribe itself can make the transition easier.

There is also a sovereignty dimension that tribal leaders tend to describe in simple terms. Names given to tribes by outsiders were frequently misspellings or approximations, as the variants Pierre listed show. Restoring the tribe's own spelling on a public-facing business is a small, visible statement about who defines the tribe. What to watch

The renaming takes effect in December. Whether the Kalispel Tribe applies Salish naming to other enterprises, as Holmes suggested, will show whether the Cusick change is a one-off or the first step in a portfolio-wide identity shift. Northern Quest, as the tribe's flagship and best-known brand, would be the most consequential test of that approach. For now, the change is concentrated at the property closest to home, in the community where the language program is being rebuilt.
