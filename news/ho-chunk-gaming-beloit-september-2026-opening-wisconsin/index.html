Ho-Chunk Gaming Beloit targets September 26 opening for $705M complex

Wisconsin's second-largest casino opens next month with 1,500 machines and a hotel deferred to 2027 — a border play aimed squarely at northern Illinois.

By Marcus WhitehorsePublished August 26, 20265 min read

The Ho-Chunk Nation's $705 million casino and entertainment complex in Beloit is entering its final month before opening, with tribal leadership targeting September 26, 2026 for the grand opening. When it opens, Ho-Chunk Gaming Beloit will become the second-largest casino in Wisconsin and the most significant new tribal gaming property to come online in the upper Midwest this decade.

Construction began in October 2024 at the southeast corner of Willowbrook and Colley roads, on land the Nation pursued through the federal fee-to-trust process for the better part of two decades. The finished first phase spans more than 240,000 square feet and is designed around roughly 1,500 gaming machines, 40 table games, a sportsbook, four restaurants and a casino bar, plus live entertainment space. Ho-Chunk Nation President Jon Greendeer has identified the late-September date as the target for the public opening.

A phased build, with the hotel following in 2027

The 200-plus-room hotel is not part of the September opening. Tribal leaders have indicated the hotel component is projected for a mid-2027 launch, and that the final room count could be revised upward depending on how the market responds in the first year of operation. That sequencing — gaming floor and food and beverage first, rooms second — has become the default structure for large tribal builds, and for good reason. The casino floor is the revenue engine; hotel rooms are a yield and length-of-stay instrument that becomes more valuable once actual visitation data exists to size it against.

The staffing ramp is correspondingly large. The property is expected to support more than 1,000 jobs, and hiring sessions have continued through August. For a project of this scale in a market the size of Beloit, workforce assembly is typically the binding constraint on the opening date rather than construction itself — a dynamic we have covered in the context of the broader tribal gaming labor squeeze.

Why Beloit, and why it matters for Wisconsin

Geography is the entire thesis. Beloit sits on the Wisconsin-Illinois state line, roughly 90 minutes from Chicago's northern suburbs and directly on the Interstate 39/90 corridor. The Ho-Chunk Nation already operates gaming facilities at Black River Falls, Wisconsin Dells, Madison, Nekoosa, Tomah and Wittenberg, but none of those properties reach into the northern Illinois catchment the way a border property does. The Nation's existing footprint is oriented toward in-state and regional traffic; Beloit is an out-of-state acquisition play.

That positioning also explains the scale. A 1,500-machine floor is not sized for Beloit's local population. It is sized for a regional draw competing against Illinois's commercial casinos — including the Rockford property roughly 20 minutes to the south — and against the Chicago-area supply that has expanded substantially since Illinois authorized additional licenses. The competitive question is not whether Beloit takes share from other Ho-Chunk properties, which it will to some degree, but whether the incremental cross-border capture exceeds that cannibalization.

Border properties are the highest-variance projects in tribal gaming. The upside is a market many times the size of the host community; the risk is that the neighboring state's own supply expands to meet the demand first.

For Wisconsin's tribal gaming market more broadly — the subject of our Wisconsin market deep dive — Beloit represents a meaningful capacity addition in a state where the compacted framework has been stable but where mobile sports betting arrangements have moved unevenly. Wisconsin's tribes operate under individually negotiated compacts with the state, and the Ho-Chunk Nation's agreement has historically been among the more contested, particularly around revenue sharing and the geographic scope of authorized gaming.

The financing structure and what it signals

The Nation secured project financing for the flagship in late 2025, and the transaction was notable for its size relative to the borrower. Tribal casino projects at the $700 million level were, until recently, the exclusive province of a handful of the largest gaming tribes. The willingness of institutional lenders to underwrite this class of project for a mid-sized operator reflects a broader repricing of tribal gaming credit that we examined in our analysis of institutional capital in tribal gaming.

It also raises the stakes on execution. Debt service on a project of this scale requires the property to perform close to its underwritten case from early in its life, which is why the September date has held firm through construction rather than slipping into the softer post-holiday window. Opening in late September puts the property into the fourth quarter — historically a solid period for Midwest regional gaming — with a full run at the winter season before the first full-year comparison.

Two variables will define the first twelve months. The first is Illinois consumer behavior: whether northern Illinois players who currently drive to Rockford or into the Chicago market will redirect to a newer, larger, tax-advantaged tribal property. The second is the hotel. Until rooms open in 2027, Beloit competes as a day-trip destination, which caps average spend per visit. The gap between the September 2026 gaming opening and the mid-2027 hotel opening is therefore the period in which the property's ceiling is artificially low — and the period in which the Nation will collect the data that determines how high to build the rest of it.
