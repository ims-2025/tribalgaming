Kickapoo Tribe Breaks Ground on Tower at Texas' Largest Casino

A hotel expansion at Lucky Eagle in Eagle Pass is a long-term bet on demand in one of the country's most legally constrained gaming states.

By Sarah BegayPublished July 25, 20264 min read

The Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas broke ground on July 15, 2026, on a new hotel tower at Lucky Eagle Casino Hotel in Eagle Pass, a major reinvestment at what remains the largest casino in Texas. The expansion signals confidence in a border-market property that has spent years operating in one of the most legally constrained gaming environments in the country — and it underscores how a single tribe can anchor a regional economy even where the rules sharply limit what games it can offer.

For a tribe operating in a state historically hostile to casino gaming, a bricks-and-mortar hotel expansion is more than a hospitality upgrade. It is a statement that Lucky Eagle's demand base is deep enough, and durable enough, to justify long-term capital.

A destination on the Texas–Mexico border

Lucky Eagle Casino Hotel sits in Eagle Pass, in Maverick County along the Rio Grande, drawing players from across South Texas and the surrounding region. The new tower adds rooms and resort amenities designed to keep visitors on property longer and to convert day-trippers into overnight guests — the same playbook driving hotel investment at tribal resorts nationwide. Keeping guests on site increases non-gaming spend on food, beverage and entertainment, a mix that has become central to tribal resort economics.

The groundbreaking also reflects the property's role as an economic anchor. As one of the region's significant employers, Lucky Eagle supports jobs, tribal government services and community programs that ripple well beyond the casino walls. Expansion projects like this one typically add construction work in the near term and permanent hospitality positions once the tower opens.

Gaming under Texas constraints

Texas does not permit Las Vegas-style Class III casino gaming, and tribes in the state have long operated in a contested legal space. The Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas has historically run its operation under the framework governing gaming permitted in the state, relying on the kinds of machines and games available absent a full tribal-state compact. That distinction — between the bingo-based electronic gaming of Class II and the broader Class III menu found in compact states — is central to understanding how Texas tribes build their businesses.

That a tribe can sustain the largest casino in Texas without a Class III compact shows how much demand exists in markets the state itself has declined to serve.

The legal backdrop matters for the expansion. Because Texas tribes cannot count on the game mix or exclusivity protections that compacts provide elsewhere, their growth depends heavily on hospitality, scale and location. A hotel tower diversifies revenue toward rooms and amenities that are insulated from the gaming-law fights that have shadowed Texas tribal operators for decades. A wider Texas and Gulf market in motion

The Eagle Pass investment lands amid renewed attention to tribal gaming in Texas and its neighbors. Legislative and legal efforts to clarify and expand what Texas tribes may offer have gained momentum, and operators across the Gulf region are competing for the same cross-border demand. Our coverage of the Texas Tribal Gaming Restoration Act and the e-bingo fight lays out the stakes for tribes seeking firmer legal footing, while our analysis of Louisiana operators chasing the Texas market shows how much revenue the state exports to neighbors.

Seen against that backdrop, the Lucky Eagle groundbreaking is a bet that the Texas market will remain robust regardless of how the legal questions resolve. By investing in rooms and resort amenities rather than waiting for a compact that may never come, the Kickapoo Traditional Tribe is building on the demand it can control today. If Texas ever does broaden what its tribes may offer, an expanded Lucky Eagle will be positioned to capitalize; if it does not, the property will still have deepened the non-gaming base that has kept it the state's largest casino.

The regional stakes are considerable. Eagle Pass and Maverick County sit in a part of South Texas where major private employers are scarce, and a resort of Lucky Eagle's scale functions as an economic anchor for the surrounding community as well as for the tribe. Hotel expansions tend to lengthen guest stays and raise per-visit spending, which in turn supports more year-round jobs than a gaming floor alone. For the Kickapoo Traditional Tribe, that translates into a larger and more stable base for funding tribal government services, from health care to education.

Timing matters too. Building rooms during a period of strong demand lets a property capture visitors it currently turns away on peak nights, and it positions the resort to compete for the conference, group and leisure travel that day-trip casinos cannot serve. In a border market where players have limited legal alternatives close to home, additional rooms can convert latent demand into overnight revenue that stays in the region rather than crossing to competitors elsewhere.

Construction is expected to proceed in phases, with the casino remaining open throughout. The tribe has framed the tower as a long-term investment in Eagle Pass and in the Kickapoo community it supports.
