Wyandotte Nation's Crosswinds Expansion: An Oklahoma Tribe Builds in Kansas

A 238-room hotel in Park City shows how an Oklahoma-based tribe grows in Kansas: through hospitality, inside a compact.

By Sarah BegayPublished October 23, 20264 min read

The Wyandotte Nation is a federally recognized tribe headquartered in Oklahoma, yet one of its most ambitious projects is rising in Park City, Kansas. The Crosswinds Casino Resort expansion, which broke ground in late June 2025 and is anticipated to be complete in summer 2027, adds a 15-story, 238-room hotel to a casino that opened in March 2021. The project raises a question that applies to a number of tribes: how does an Oklahoma-based nation run a Kansas casino, and what does that mean for its growth strategy?

The expansion in detail

According to the Nation, the Four Diamond-targeted hotel will include 32 suites, a pool with an outdoor bar and party area, a fitness center, private meeting rooms and an 8,500-square-foot ballroom with pre-function space. The casino floor will be more spacious, with more than 1,400 slot machines, defined smoking and non-smoking sections, a high-limit room and a VIP lounge. Additional amenities include a tap house and sports bar with a large video wall, a food hall, a coffee shop, a center bar, a gift shop and a rewards center. The Nation expects 350 new team members.

Wyandotte Nation Chief Billy Friend described the expansion as "an opportunity for the Wyandotte Nation to enhance its economic standing," framed as supporting community needs and cultural heritage. Kelly Carpino, CEO of WTOK, the Nation's economic development division, said the hotel adds a "stay and play" opportunity. Crosswinds is the Nation's fourth casino, joining 7th Street Casino in Kansas City, Kansas, and River Bend Casino and Hotel and Lucky Turtle Casino in Oklahoma.

Why a tribe's gaming sits where its land does

Under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act, tribes may conduct gaming on Indian lands, and the legal pathway depends on the class of game. Class III gaming, which includes slot machines and table games, requires a tribal-state compact. Where a tribe's gaming lands lie in a state other than its home state, the compact is negotiated with the state where the facility sits. For tribes like the Wyandotte Nation, whose historic ties extend to Kansas, that means working within Kansas's framework even as the tribal government is based in Oklahoma. Our explainer on the IGRA definition of Indian lands sets out why the location of the land governs which state's rules apply.

Because the Nation's Kansas gaming is governed by its own compact arrangements, the compact status is a recurring theme in coverage of its operations. Our report on the Nation's Class III compact and operation of law describes how that arrangement came about, and the mechanics of the 45-day review are covered in our operation-of-law explainer.

Where a compact defines the permitted games, the hotel is the part of the project the compact does not have to touch.

The strategic logic of a hotel

For a tribe operating under a compact that sets the scope of games, a hotel is attractive precisely because it sits outside the compact's gaming provisions. Rooms, a ballroom and restaurants are ordinary hospitality assets. They can be financed, staffed and marketed without revisiting what games are authorized, and they diversify revenue. That logic is visible in the Crosswinds design, which devotes a large share of the program to non-gaming space. We compare it with other projects in our analysis of hotel-to-slot ratios at three tribal resorts.

The tribe's Oklahoma identity also matters. The Nation describes Crosswinds as maintaining a focus on Oklahoma-style gaming, a phrase that signals the product mix and service style the Nation is aiming for in the Wichita-area market. Readers interested in the wider state picture can consult our Kansas market deep-dive.

Sports betting and other open questions

Industry coverage has noted that the Nation has said it would not offer sports betting at Crosswinds without a compact provision with Kansas that permits it. We reference that report only as a pointer: the scope of games at any tribal casino is defined by its compact, and adding a new category of wagering generally requires an amendment or new agreement. Our analysis of the compact bottleneck for tribal sports betting covers why legal authorization, not technology, is often the limiting step.

Other questions remain open in the public record. The Nation did not state a project cost in the announcement materials reviewed for this report, and TribalGaming.com is not estimating one. Completion remains anticipated for summer 2027, and construction schedules for projects of this size can shift for reasons ranging from weather to supply chains, a theme explored in our piece on why opening dates slip.

What the project shows is a tribal government investing in a facility outside its home state and doing so through hospitality. For tribes whose lands are distant from their headquarters, that combination of legal clarity and non-gaming growth is a durable template, and Crosswinds will be one of the better-documented tests of it when the hotel opens.
