Hard Rock Casino Tejon Breaks Ground on $725 Million Hotel Expansion

The Tejon Indian Tribe and Hard Rock start a 406-room hotel build less than a year after the Kern County casino opened.

By Elena RuizPublished October 23, 20264 min read

The Tejon Indian Tribe and Hard Rock International broke ground on October 22, 2026, on the next phase of Hard Rock Casino Tejon, a hotel-led expansion with a stated investment of more than $725 million. The property sits on tribal land in Mettler, California, just under 15 miles south of Bakersfield, and the partners expect the expanded resort to open in summer 2028 under the name Hard Rock Hotel & Casino Tejon. For the Kern County market and for California tribal gaming more broadly, the groundbreaking is a marker of how quickly a newly opened casino can move into a full resort build-out.

The original casino opened in November 2025, which means the tribe is starting its largest construction phase roughly eleven months after first opening its doors. That pace is the story. Tribal operators have long followed a sequence of opening a gaming-first facility, proving the market, and then financing the amenities that convert a regional casino into a destination. At Tejon, the interval between the two steps has been unusually short.

What the expansion includes

The centerpiece is a 406-room Hard Rock Hotel, including 119 suites, with king and double-queen room types, resort pools and a Rock Spa that will have 13 treatment rooms. The gaming floor grows by 900 slot machines, bringing the total to 3,000. Dining expands with a Council Oak Steaks & Seafood restaurant, two additional outlets in a space called The Marketplace, and a new Center Bar. The project also adds convention and event space, including three smaller meeting rooms, pre-function space and an outdoor event lawn, along with two resort-style pools with cabanas and daybeds.

Employment figures give a sense of scale. The partners project more than 1,500 construction jobs during the build and 350 new permanent positions at completion, which would bring total employment at the property to more than 1,700. Construction began immediately after the ceremony.

A tribal milestone, in the tribe's words

Tejon Indian Tribe Chairman Octavio Escobedo III described the day as a "historic milestone for the Tejon Indian Tribe." Mike Bean, property president of Hard Rock Casino Tejon, said the property has quickly become "a premier gaming and entertainment destination in California" and that the hotel and added amenities will help elevate the guest experience and draw visitors from across the state and beyond.

The hotel, not the slot count, is what changes the property's competitive set: it turns a drive-in casino into an overnight destination.

That observation reflects the economics behind the project. Slot additions deepen the gaming floor, but a 406-room hotel extends the length of stay, supports non-gaming spending on dining, spa and events, and allows the property to compete for visitors who would otherwise treat Bakersfield or a Los Angeles-area resort as their destination. Our earlier coverage of the phase-two plan for the Kern County resort laid out the project as it was first described; today's groundbreaking moves it from plan to construction.

Where Tejon fits in California's pipeline

California remains the largest tribal gaming market in the country, and the state is in the middle of an unusually heavy construction cycle. Tejon's expansion joins a group of projects that tribal operators and their partners are carrying forward at the same time, which raises practical questions about labor, materials and financing that we examined in our analysis of the open-then-expand pattern. Developers who sequence projects this way can fund later phases from the cash flow of earlier ones, while also learning from actual customer data rather than market studies alone.

For readers tracking the state as a whole, the California state hub lists tribal casinos and operators across the state, and our reporting on the wave of tribal hotel towers places Tejon alongside similar projects elsewhere in Indian Country.

What to watch

Three questions will shape how this expansion performs. The first is construction execution: a build of this size over roughly two years depends on contractor capacity and material costs, both of which have been tight across the sector. The second is market response. A larger hotel in a market south of Bakersfield will test how much overnight demand exists beyond the existing day-trip base. The third is regulatory continuity: the project proceeds under the tribe's existing gaming framework, and any change to scope of games or slot allocations would be a matter for the tribe and the state.

The partners have not, in the materials reviewed for this report, described the financing structure of the $725 million-plus investment, and TribalGaming.com is not characterizing it here. What is clear is that the tribe and Hard Rock have committed to a summer 2028 delivery target, giving observers a concrete benchmark against which to measure progress over the next two years.

The groundbreaking is a reminder that for many tribal governments, a casino opening is the start of a development sequence rather than its conclusion. Gaming revenue funds government services and further investment, and at Tejon that reinvestment cycle is now visible in steel and concrete. We will follow the project through its major construction milestones.
