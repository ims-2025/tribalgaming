SIGA Picks Bede Gaming to Build BetSIGA, Replacing PlayNow in 2027

Saskatchewan's First Nations gaming authority is trading a shared brand for an online platform it calls its own.

By Linda CardinalPublished November 12, 20264 min read

The Saskatchewan Indian Gaming Authority has selected Bede Gaming as the technology partner for BetSIGA, a new online gaming platform that SIGA plans to launch in spring 2027 as the replacement for PlayNow, the province's only legally recognized online gaming site. The announcement, made November 3 in Saskatoon, follows a competitive procurement process and marks the next step in a digital strategy the First Nations gaming authority has been building since it began operating online gaming in Saskatchewan in November 2022.

"This will be our own platform," SIGA president and CEO Zane Hansen said, describing BetSIGA as "an important next step in SIGA's long-term digital gaming strategy." Bede Gaming chief executive Colin Cole-Johnson said the partnership "extends far beyond technology implementation."

What BetSIGA will be

According to SIGA, BetSIGA will be available through a responsive website and dedicated iOS and Android apps, and will be built on open, modular technology intended to support data, loyalty and personalization features over time. The authority listed identity verification, geolocation, anti-money laundering controls, security, player protection and responsible gaming tools among the platform's core features. Existing PlayNow players in Saskatchewan will be migrated to the new site, which SIGA says will be customized for players in the province. SIGA has not published a date for retiring PlayNow or details of how the migration will work.

Revenue from BetSIGA will continue to be distributed to First Nation communities and the province. SIGA is a non-profit corporation, and net profits from its operations flow to First Nation communities, community development corporations and Saskatchewan's general revenue fund under the province's Gaming Framework Agreement. Our report on SIGA's record 2025-26 results details how that distribution works in practice, with half of net income directed to the First Nations Trust.

Why the Bede Gaming selection matters

SIGA's Saskatchewan site has operated under the PlayNow name, the same brand the British Columbia Lottery Corporation uses for its own online gaming site. Moving to BetSIGA gives the authority a platform and brand that are its own, a change Hansen emphasized in his remarks. The selection of Bede Gaming, a U.K.-based online gaming platform provider, means SIGA will run the customer-facing operation while relying on a technology partner for the underlying platform. That is a common arrangement among lottery corporations and Indigenous operators that want operational control without developing core software in-house.

The timing is tied to the province's policy calendar. On August 31, 2026, Saskatchewan and the Federation of Sovereign Indigenous Nations amended the Gaming Framework Agreement to extend SIGA's exclusivity as the province's online gaming provider through September 2029, as we reported in our coverage of the exclusivity extension and the Flying Dust pilot. A launch in spring 2027 would leave roughly two and a half years of guaranteed exclusivity in which to recover the cost of a new platform, build a customer base and demonstrate results ahead of the next negotiation.

A distinct model in Canada's online market

Saskatchewan's approach, in which a single First Nations-owned authority holds exclusive online rights under a government-to-government framework, contrasts with the open, multi-operator markets in Ontario and, since 2026, Alberta, where First Nations have pursued carve-outs, revenue sharing or direct licensing. We compared those structures in our analysis of closed versus open iGaming markets. The BetSIGA decision reinforces the Saskatchewan model by investing in it rather than treating the current arrangement as a placeholder.

The commercial challenge is unchanged. Saskatchewan is a small market by population, and SIGA's main online competition is not a licensed rival but the offshore sites that Canadian players have long been able to reach. A platform that is easier to use, offers broader content and personalizes the experience is the main lever a regulated provider has against that grey market. The responsible gaming features SIGA listed are both regulatory obligations and a point of distinction from unregulated sites, which typically offer neither provincial oversight nor a path for proceeds to return to communities.

SIGA frames BetSIGA as owned infrastructure: a platform built with a technology partner but run by a First Nations institution, with proceeds returning to communities under the Gaming Framework Agreement.

The decision also fits a broader pattern of First Nations institutions taking direct ownership positions across Canadian gaming, from casino acquisitions in British Columbia to Alberta's largest casino operator coming under First Nations ownership. SIGA's own land-based growth is summarized in our earlier report on the authority's expansion, and the online platform now becomes a parallel track for the same mandate.

What to watch before launch

Several details remain to be announced: the date PlayNow will be retired in Saskatchewan, how player accounts, balances and loyalty standing will carry over, which game suppliers will be integrated, and whether BetSIGA will add products beyond those PlayNow offers today. SIGA's next annual report should also show how much of the authority's income now comes from online play, a figure that will matter when the province and the FSIN revisit the exclusivity terms before 2029. For now, the Bede Gaming selection settles the technology question and sets a spring 2027 target for the most significant change to Saskatchewan's online gaming market since 2022.
