Kickapoo Lucky Eagle Hotel Expansion Has No Opening Date as Texas Shifts

The 252-room project is moving quietly, while the election that unseated Dan Patrick puts Texas tribal gaming back on the legislative calendar.

By Daniel YazziePublished November 20, 20265 min read

More than four months after the Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas broke ground on a hotel expansion at Lucky Eagle Casino Hotel in Eagle Pass, the project still has no public opening date. As of November 1, 2026, the tribe had not announced a completion target, named a general contractor, or reported construction milestones, according to a project-status review published by 500 Nations. That kind of silence is common for tribal construction projects, and on its own it is not a sign of trouble. What has changed is the political ground around the Kickapoo Lucky Eagle hotel expansion. The November 3 election reshaped Texas leadership, and the 2027 legislative session is already drawing gaming bills.

What the Kickapoo Lucky Eagle hotel expansion includes

The tribe broke ground on July 15, 2026, as we reported in our coverage of the tower groundbreaking. Public descriptions of the project call for 252 new rooms, 80 of them suites. Plans also include a resort-style pool, a new restaurant and bar, courtyard improvements and a renovated lobby. The tribe has not released a budget.

Lucky Eagle, in Maverick County on the Rio Grande, is widely described as the largest casino in Texas. Its location across the Rio Grande from Piedras Negras positions it to draw from South Texas and northern Mexico alike. A larger hotel serves the same goal as resort expansions elsewhere: turning day-trippers into overnight guests and adding spending on rooms, food and drink that does not depend on the gaming floor.

The lack of a public schedule is not unusual. Tribal enterprises are governmental businesses, not public companies, and they have no disclosure obligation comparable to securities filings. Many announce opening dates only once interiors are underway. Still, the comparison with other 2026 projects stands out. Tribes such as the Catawba Nation and the Pascua Yaqui Tribe have released schedules and milestones throughout construction. In Eagle Pass, observers have little to go on beyond the groundbreaking itself.

The legal track Kickapoo operates on

The Kickapoo situation in Texas differs from that of the state's other two federally recognized tribes. The Ysleta del Sur Pueblo and the Alabama-Coushatta Tribe of Texas are governed by the 1987 Restoration Act, which the U.S. Supreme Court interpreted in 2022. In Ysleta del Sur Pueblo v. Texas, the Court held that the act bars only gaming that Texas prohibits outright, not gaming the state regulates. That ruling opened the door for electronic bingo at both tribes' properties, including the Alabama-Coushatta's temporary Naskila Casino Leggett, which opened August 25 on the future site of a permanent resort expected to open in phases starting in late 2028.

The Kickapoo, by contrast, game under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act. Without a tribal-state compact, Lucky Eagle offers Class II gaming, meaning bingo and bingo-based electronic machines, rather than the Class III slots and house-banked table games found in compact states. The tribe tried for years to secure a Class III alternative. In the 2000s it pursued federal secretarial procedures after Texas declined to negotiate, but the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit invalidated Interior's procedures regulation in Texas v. United States in 2007. Texas has never signed a Class III compact with any tribe. The TribalGaming.com Legal Guide explains the difference between the compact and procedures routes.

A hotel tower is one of the few growth levers a Class II operator in Texas fully controls. It does not depend on a compact the state has never offered.

A new legislative calendar for Texas tribes

The 2026 election changed the people who will decide whether that ever happens. Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick, who presides over the Texas Senate and has long blocked gaming expansion, lost his race to Vikki Goodwin, according to post-election reporting. Gina Hinojosa was elected governor. Under IGRA, Class III compacts are negotiated by the state, and in most states the governor leads that process. Our analysis of the 2027 class of governors-elect looks at what the change could mean for tribes in Texas and elsewhere.

Bills followed quickly. On November 9, the first day of pre-filing for the 2027 session, State Rep. Charlie Geren of Fort Worth filed HJR 52. The joint resolution would let voters decide in November 2027 whether to authorize casino gaming and sports wagering, capped at 10 casino licenses with initial terms of at least 40 years. It would also set up a process for Class III compact negotiations with the three tribes, as we detailed in our report on HJR 52 and tribal compacts. State Sen. Carol Alvarado has reportedly filed similar legislation in the Senate.

The numbers remain difficult. A constitutional amendment needs a two-thirds vote in both chambers before it reaches the ballot, and the House-passed sports betting amendment of 2023 stalled in the Senate. A new presiding officer changes who controls the Senate agenda, but it does not by itself supply the votes. The 90th Legislature convenes on January 12, 2027.

What it means for Eagle Pass

For the Kickapoo, the hotel project and the legislative debate run on separate timelines, and the tribe has kept them separate. The expansion was planned and started under the current Class II framework, and it makes sense on that basis alone. If the Legislature eventually sends voters a casino amendment that includes a compact pathway, Lucky Eagle would enter that period with more rooms and amenities than any competitor could build quickly. If no amendment passes, the tribe will still have expanded the parts of its business that Texas law does not restrict.

The next signs to watch are practical: a named contractor, a topping-out, or a target season for the first new rooms. TribalGaming.com will report those as the tribe makes them public.
