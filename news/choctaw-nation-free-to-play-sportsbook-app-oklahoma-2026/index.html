Choctaw Nation Runs a Free-to-Play Sportsbook While Oklahoma Waits

A sportsbook that cannot take a bet is still an acquisition engine — and Choctaw is not the only tribe that knows it.

By Marcus WhitehorsePublished September 20, 20265 min read

The Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma is running a sportsbook that cannot take a bet. Choctaw Sports, the free-to-play prediction app the tribe's gaming enterprise launched this summer, lets users make picks across a dozen sports using daily credits, with no cash in and no cash out. It is a functioning sportsbook product in a state where Oklahoma tribal sports betting remains illegal — and that is precisely the point.

The app arrives after another legislative session ended without a sports wagering framework, leaving Oklahoma among the shrinking group of states with no legal channel despite hosting one of the largest tribal gaming markets in the country. For the Choctaw Nation, which operates properties across southeastern Oklahoma including Durant, Pocola, Hochatown, Idabel, McAlester, Broken Bow, Grant and Stringtown, the calculation is straightforward: build the audience now, monetize it if and when the law changes.

What the product actually does

Choctaw Sports gives users free daily login credits to predict outcomes across football, basketball, baseball, hockey, soccer, golf, tennis, table tennis, cricket, Formula 1, UFC/MMA and volleyball. Unlike much of the free-to-play category, it offers no in-app purchases and no paid credit top-ups — there is no revenue line inside the app at all. It is restricted to users 21 and older and available for download nationwide.

The platform behind it is Kambi, the Malta-based turnkey sportsbook supplier that already powers a substantial share of tribal retail and mobile sportsbooks in the United States. That choice is the most revealing detail in the launch. Running a free-to-play product on a full commercial sportsbook stack means the odds feed, the bet-slip mechanics, the settlement logic and the trading operation are the same ones that would run a real-money book. The conversion work, if Oklahoma ever legalizes, is largely a matter of switching on payments and compliance rather than rebuilding a product.

Tom McDonald, senior director of digital gaming for Choctaw Casinos & Resorts, described the app as a way for people to learn how sports betting works without risk. That framing is accurate and also incomplete. A free-to-play book is a customer acquisition instrument: it generates a registered, age-verified, opted-in user base with known sport preferences and engagement patterns, assembled at zero regulatory cost in a jurisdiction where a licensed operator could not legally acquire a single customer.

The Oklahoma standoff behind the strategy

Oklahoma's impasse is structural rather than technical. The state's tribes hold Class III gaming under compacts that define authorized games, and sports wagering is not among them. Adding it requires either compact amendments or a new statutory framework, and both routes run through a governor's office that has argued the state should open any future sports betting market to commercial operators rather than reserving it for tribal nations. Tribal leaders have consistently rejected that premise, treating exclusivity as the consideration they already paid for through years of revenue sharing.

The result is a market where roughly three dozen tribes operate more than 140 gaming facilities and none can take a legal sports wager. Our coverage of the collapse of HB 1047 and the pivot that followed traced how close the legislature came and where it broke down. The Choctaw Nation is now positioning for the next attempt rather than the last one.

A free-to-play book does not generate revenue. It generates a list. In a state where legal acquisition is impossible, the list is the asset.

Bet Choctaw and the on-property workaround

The more consequential piece of the tribe's digital roadmap is Bet Choctaw, a real-money online gaming app the enterprise says will function for users physically located on Choctaw casino property. That is the on-reservation geofenced model that several tribes have used to extend digital play without a statewide authorization: the wager is placed and accepted on Indian lands, within the scope of games the compact already permits, with geolocation enforcing the boundary.

The approach is legally conservative compared with statewide mobile, which generally requires either a compact amendment adopting a server-location theory or state legislation. It is also commercially modest — handle is capped by footfall. But it lets a tribal enterprise build digital operating competence, payment infrastructure, responsible gaming tooling and player accounts inside the compact it already holds. Tribes that have gone this route generally describe it as preparation rather than destination. The mechanics of the broader statewide model are set out in our explainer on hub-and-spoke tribal sports betting.

Choctaw has been assembling these pieces for some time. Its social slots app, Choctaw Slots, has run since 2022, giving the enterprise four years of data on how its land-based customers behave in a digital environment and how much cross-promotion actually drives property visits. Choctaw Sports extends that dataset into a vertical the tribe cannot yet sell.

What to watch

Three things will determine whether the strategy pays. The first is whether the 2027 legislative session produces a framework, and on what terms — a tribal-exclusive model, a hybrid with commercial operators, or another adjournment. The second is how large the registered user base gets; a free-to-play book with a few thousand users is a marketing curiosity, while one with several hundred thousand is a genuine head start on day one of a legal market.

The third is competitive. Other Oklahoma operators have the same incentive and access to the same suppliers, and the tribe that assembles the largest pre-legalization database will enter any future market with the lowest acquisition cost. That dynamic tends to accelerate once one operator moves. Readers tracking the state's operators and properties can start with the Oklahoma directory and our profile of Choctaw Casinos & Resorts.

For now, Choctaw Sports is a book that pays nothing and costs its users nothing. It is also the most concrete indication yet that Oklahoma's largest gaming operators have stopped waiting for the legislature to move before they build.
