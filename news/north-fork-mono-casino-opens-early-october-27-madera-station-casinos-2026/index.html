North Fork Mono Casino Opens Early in Madera as Court Ruling Awaits

The Madera County casino opened a day ahead of schedule with more than 2,400 slots and 40 tables, even as a neighboring tribe's federal suit remains undecided.

By Sarah BegayPublished October 28, 20264 min read

The North Fork Mono Casino in Madera County, California, opened its doors on October 27, 2026, a day ahead of the date that had been widely reported. The North Fork Rancheria of Mono Indians and its management partner, Station Casinos, held the opening at 3:00 p.m., and the operator's announcement described it as an unexpected early opening. The North Fork Mono Casino opening ends a development effort that began roughly two decades ago and now moves the tribe's focus to operations.

The property sits off Highway 99 in California's Central Valley. Local coverage described a ribbon-cutting attended by hundreds of people, with tribal members among the first inside. The Highway Patrol warned of traffic delays near the site, directing northbound drivers to exit at Avenue 12 and southbound drivers at Avenue 18 1/2.

What opened on October 27

According to the operator, the facility spans about 264,000 square feet with floor-to-ceiling windows. The gaming floor has more than 2,400 slot machines and 40 table games, including a high-limit room with 138 slots, ten table games, a private bar, and a dedicated cashier. Earlier reporting had cited a somewhat smaller slot count of more than 2,000, so the final figure appears to have been set late in the build.

The property has eight dining venues and four bars. The announcement highlighted Manzanita Steakhouse, which lists more than 250 local and international wine labels curated by a master sommelier, and Clubhouse 99, a sports-oriented bar with 56 large-format screens. Local reporting also noted a food court, and said future phases include a gas station and a hotel. The operator projects more than 1,500 local jobs across gaming, hospitality, and food and beverage.

The reported cost is not consistent across sources. The operator's own announcement gives an approximate all-in project cost of $750 million in one passage and a $725 million development in another, a gap that may reflect different definitions of what counts toward the total. Our earlier reporting used the $725 million figure, and our coverage of the project financing explains how the capital was structured.

The early start also gives the operator several extra weeks of data before the holiday season, when regional casino traffic typically rises. Whether the move reflects construction readiness, staffing, or simply a decision to open once the building was ready has not been explained publicly, and the announcement does not say.

An opening with a lawsuit still pending

The early opening arrives while a federal case remains undecided. Picayune Rancheria of the Chukchansi Indians, a neighboring tribe, sued the chair of the National Indian Gaming Commission in June, challenging the Commission's approval of North Fork's 2024 management contract with Station Casinos. The case is before U.S. District Judge Kirk E. Sherriff. Local reporting on the opening noted that a ruling in the case is still pending, and that neighboring tribes had made several attempts to halt the project over the years.

The distinction between the casino and the contract matters. The suit targets the federal approval of a management arrangement rather than the existence of the building, and what relief a court could grant after operations begin is an open question. Our report on the pending ruling and management contract sets out the legal theory, and the Picayune lawsuit background traces how the dispute developed. For readers who want to understand what a management contract is and why the federal government reviews it, see our explainer on IGRA management contracts.

Why the opening matters for California's market

North Fork's 2,400-plus machines place it among the larger tribal gaming floors in the Central Valley, a region where existing tribal properties already compete for the same customers along the Highway 99 corridor. Regional competitors are also investing: our coverage of the Eagle Mountain expansion in Porterville shows how other tribes are expanding ahead of 2027. How the new property affects play at those venues will become visible only after several months of reported results, and nothing in the announcement quantifies it.

The ownership structure is also worth noting. The tribe says it has more than 3,000 members and describes itself as California's fifth-largest tribe by that measure, and tribal leaders framed the casino as a path to economic self-sufficiency. Tribe Leader Elaine Bethel Fink told the crowd, "This is our gambling house." Station Casinos, a subsidiary of Red Rock Resorts, manages the property in partnership with the tribe, which is the arrangement the pending case targets.

What to watch next

Three items will clarify the picture. The first is the court's ruling on the claims against the Commission's approval and whether any party seeks interim relief. The second is early operating results, which Red Rock Resorts may address when it reports earnings. The third is progress on the planned hotel and fuel station, which would extend the project beyond its first phase.
