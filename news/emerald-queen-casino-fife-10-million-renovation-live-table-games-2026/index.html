Emerald Queen Casino Fife Finishes $10M Renovation, Adds Live Tables

A year-long refresh gives the Puyallup Tribe's older South Sound casino a table pit and a distinct role beside the Tacoma flagship.

By Marcus WhitehorsePublished November 27, 20265 min read

The Puyallup Tribe of Indians has completed a $10 million renovation of Emerald Queen Casino Fife, the smaller of the tribe's two Pierce County gaming properties, and with it the Fife location is getting something it has never offered: live table games. The project, which took about a year and finished ahead of schedule according to local coverage in September, refreshed the gaming floor, modernized the guest entrance and replaced core building systems. It also marks a shift in how the tribe positions the two properties that carry the Emerald Queen name.

The Fife casino opened in December 2004 inside a former Best Western hotel along the Interstate 5 corridor east of Tacoma. For most of its history it has functioned as a slot-focused companion to the larger Tacoma property. Adding blackjack, roulette and a Chase the Flush poker-style game gives it a table pit for the first time, even if a small one compared with the more than 60 tables at the Tacoma casino.

What the Emerald Queen Casino Fife renovation changed

According to Weekly Volcano's report on the project, which cited EQC marketing production manager Justin Center, the work covered a refreshed gaming floor, a new entrance, upgraded HVAC, new carpeting, updated lighting and a set of guest amenity improvements. The on-site Tatoosh Grill received a revised menu, and a new restaurant, 2102 Tavern, is tentatively planned for 2027. The tribe expected to hold a grand reopening in late September or early October, timed to coincide with Emerald Queen's 30th anniversary as a brand.

None of those items is unusual on its own. Mid-cycle refreshes of this size are routine for regional casinos that are two decades old. What makes the Fife project worth watching is the decision to put live dealers on a floor that has operated without them. Tables carry higher labor costs per square foot than slots, and tribal operators across the Pacific Northwest have spent the past several years managing dealer shortages. That pressure is examined in our analysis of dealer workforce constraints at tribal casinos. A modest pit at Fife suggests the tribe sees enough local demand to justify the staffing, and that it would rather capture that play close to home than send every table customer to Tacoma.

A two-property strategy in a crowded South Sound market

The Puyallup Tribe has been in the gaming business since the 1990s. Its original Emerald Queen was a converted Mississippi-style riverboat on the Tacoma waterfront before the tribe moved gaming to land along Interstate 5. In June 2020, at the start of the pandemic, the tribe opened the current Emerald Queen Casino in Tacoma, a large resort-style building that replaced the older casino on the same corridor. Casino.org reported at the time that the project cost roughly $400 million and included more than 2,100 slot machines, about 60 table games and a 155-room hotel.

The tribe has since outlined plans to redevelop the roughly 17.6-acre site of the former Tacoma casino into a mixed-use district. A 2023 report described a first phase that would include a tribal administration building, a parking structure and a museum on Puyallup history, with retail, dining and possibly housing in later phases. That plan shows how a tribal government can use gaming land for purposes beyond gaming once a casino moves.

The South Sound is one of the most competitive tribal gaming markets in the country. Within driving distance of Fife are properties operated by the Muckleshoot, Nisqually, Squaxin Island, Chehalis and Snoqualmie tribes, among others, and Washington's compact framework allows each of them to offer a similar mix of electronic and table games plus on-premises sports wagering. Our Pacific Northwest tribal gaming deep dive covers the full regional landscape. In that environment, keeping an older second property current is a defensive move as much as a growth one. A tired building cedes local customers to newer competitors, and those customers may not come back.

Compact context and the Washington framework

Washington's tribal-state compacts, negotiated with the Washington State Gambling Commission and approved by the Interior Department, govern the number and type of games each tribe may operate, including table limits and the electronic "tribal lottery system" terminals that make up most of the state's machine inventory. Over the past two years a number of tribes have amended their compacts to address electronic table games, wager limits and sports wagering details. Several of those changes are summarized in our coverage of the Washington Class III compact amendments wave. The Fife table expansion does not appear to require any new compact authority, since the Puyallup compact already permits table games at the tribe's facilities. It is an operating choice within an existing framework, not a regulatory change.

For tribal operators with more than one property, the question is rarely whether to reinvest in the smaller casino, but how to give it a distinct role so the two locations complement each other rather than compete.

That is the strategic logic behind Fife's makeover. A slot-heavy neighborhood casino with a handful of tables, a refreshed grill and a new tavern on the way serves a different visit than a resort with a hotel, a large pit and concert programming. The Puyallup approach mirrors what other multi-property tribes in Washington and elsewhere have done: concentrate destination amenities at the flagship and make the second site faster and more convenient for regulars.

What to watch next

Three things will show whether the investment pays off. The first is whether the Fife table games hold their staffing through the busy holiday season. The second is the timing of 2102 Tavern, which the tribe has described only as tentatively planned for 2027. The third is progress on the former Tacoma casino site, which would give the Puyallup Tribe a civic and cultural anchor on the same corridor as its two casinos.

Operators and regulators following Washington tribal gaming can find property listings and compact summaries on our Washington state hub. For now, the Fife renovation is a reminder that much of tribal gaming's capital spending goes not into new resorts but into keeping existing properties competitive.
