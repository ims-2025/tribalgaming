Tulalip Completes Three-Year Casino Expansion: What a 35% Larger Floor Signals

A 270,250-square-foot floor and more than 2,600 slots mark the biggest investment in Tulalip's gaming facilities since 2003.

By James KingfisherPublished October 15, 20264 min read

Tulalip Resort Casino announced on October 1 that it has completed a three-year expansion and renovation, a project the tribal gaming organization's president describes as the most significant financial commitment to its gaming facilities since the casino opened in 2003. The finished work adds 70,250 square feet, a 35 percent increase, bringing the gaming floor to 270,250 square feet and the slot count to more than 2,600. For the tribal gaming industry, the Tulalip project is a useful case study in how a mature, high-performing Pacific Northwest property decides to reinvest, and what a multi-year expansion looks like when the casino stays open throughout.

What was built

According to reporting by The Herald of Everett, the project began with construction in January 2024, following an expansion plan announced in November 2023 to mark the casino's 20th anniversary. The 70,250-square-foot addition opened to the public in July 2025, and renovation of the remaining floor followed. The expansion added roughly 400 slot machines and includes a renovated high-limit slots room, a new North Bar, an outdoor covered patio and a gourmet ice cream shop called Salish Sweets. A signature Center Bar stands 38 feet tall and weighs seven tons. Design themes draw on the Salish Sea, the coastline and the forests, with blue lighting meant to evoke water currents running north to south and a ceiling light display.

Tulalip Gaming Organization has not disclosed the project's cost. That is typical: tribal enterprises are not publicly traded and generally release financial figures selectively. Marci Fryberg, the organization's president, characterized the investment as the largest since the 2003 opening, which suggests a scale well beyond routine refreshes. The architecture firm Rice Fergus Miller, whose hospitality studio led design, described the renovation as creating "a unified yet diverse gaming floor," according to the Herald.

Why reinvest in the floor

The decision to expand the gaming floor itself, rather than only add hotel rooms or entertainment space, reflects a view of where demand sits. Tribal casinos in the Puget Sound region compete in a dense market, and a property that has operated for more than two decades faces both physical aging and shifting customer expectations. Our analysis of the aging slot floor and the shift toward experiential play describes the pressure that long-running properties face to modernize layouts, lighting and amenities simultaneously.

A 35 percent increase in floor area, paired with 400 added machines, also points to a calculation about capacity. If a floor consistently runs near full on peak days, adding positions can raise revenue without needing to win new customers. The reverse risk is dilution: more machines spread the same play across more positions and lower the average daily win per unit. Understanding that tradeoff requires metrics such as coin-in and hold, which we explain in our primer on handle, coin-in and hold. Tulalip has not released performance data on the expanded floor, so any conclusion about returns would be premature.

The balance between the gaming floor and non-gaming amenities is a recurring theme across the industry. The new bars, patio and food offerings at Tulalip fit a pattern we describe in our coverage of food and beverage as a driver of casino visits. Amenities that keep guests on property longer can support gaming revenue even when they do not themselves generate large profits.

Phased construction while open

One of the more instructive aspects of the project is its length and sequencing. A three-year timeline with the expansion opening roughly midway through allowed the casino to bring new capacity online before completing renovation of existing space. That sequencing is a standard approach for properties that cannot afford extended closures, since lost gaming revenue during a shutdown would be substantial. We examine the logistics and capital planning in our analysis of renovating while open.

The tradeoff is disruption. Construction adjacent to an active floor requires barriers, rerouted traffic, noise and dust control, and careful management of regulatory requirements such as surveillance coverage and cage access. Tribal gaming regulators must approve changes to floor layouts and internal controls, and machine moves must comply with the minimum internal control standards the tribe has adopted. Those requirements add planning time, though the Herald's account does not detail how Tulalip handled them.

Context in the Washington market

Tulalip operates under a Class III compact with the State of Washington, one of several that have been amended repeatedly in recent years. Our coverage of the tribe's own compact talks is in the tentative amendment agreement, and our Washington state hub lists tribal gaming facilities across the state. The tribe's broader enterprise portfolio, including the Quil Ceda Village commercial district, is profiled in our Tulalip enterprise profile.

The expansion also arrives at a time of regulatory uncertainty around competition from prediction markets, which Tulalip leaders have been vocal about. Glen Gobin of the Tulalip Tribes council has lobbied federal regulators for a ban on sports event contracts, and CNN has reported that Tulalip casino funds helped pay for a $67 million water pipeline and a $19 million early education center. Capital investments like the floor expansion are made against that backdrop: a tribe committing substantial funds to physical infrastructure is betting that in-person play remains a durable revenue base.

Takeaways

Three points stand out. First, long-established tribal casinos continue to reinvest heavily rather than treat their original footprints as finished. Second, floor-focused expansion remains a primary strategy even as operators add digital products. Third, the absence of disclosed financial results is a reminder of how limited public visibility into tribal gaming performance can be. Until Tulalip or regulators release data, the project's success will be inferred from the scale of the commitment rather than measured by published returns.
