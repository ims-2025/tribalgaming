Ho-Chunk Beloit Grand Opening Set for Oct. 23: What Phase One Includes

After a three-day-notice delay from Sept. 26, the Ho-Chunk Nation's Wisconsin–Illinois border casino opens as a casino-first phase, with the hotel and convention center to follow in fall 2027.

By Marcus WhitehorsePublished October 20, 20265 min read

The Ho-Chunk Nation will open Ho-Chunk Gaming Beloit on Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, bringing a long-planned tribal casino to the Wisconsin–Illinois state line after more than a decade of development. The grand opening was originally scheduled for Sept. 26; the Nation announced the new date on Sept. 23, three days before the original one, and did not publicly give a reason for the change.

Nation President Jon Greendeer announced the new date, tying the project to both the area's history and its future. The Ho-Chunk Nation says the Beloit project has been in development since 2012, and the site carries historical significance for the Ho-Chunk people, who know the village that once stood in the area as Keecak.

What Ho-Chunk Gaming Beloit opens with on Oct. 23

Phase one is a casino-first opening. The property will operate around the clock with 1,300 slot machines, a figure more specific than some earlier plans for the gaming floor suggested. Table games will include blackjack, 3-Card Poker, Mississippi Stud, Ultimate Texas Hold'Em, roulette, craps, baccarat and I Love Suits, alongside live Hold'Em poker.

Food and entertainment at opening are built around four outlets: Draft Pick Pub House, Black River Coffee Shop, Livewire Lounge and Food Truck Alley. A parking garage is part of the first phase, which matters in a border market where drive-in traffic from northern Illinois is central to the business case. The Nation has said the grand opening will include entertainment, Ho-Chunk cultural programming and giveaways, though a detailed schedule had not been released at the time of the announcement.

Notably, the hotel is not part of phase one. The hotel and convention center are now scheduled for fall 2027, and their completion will mark the opening of the full resort. At full build-out the Nation expects to employ about 1,500 people at the property. The Nation has not published a construction cost in the materials reviewed for this article, so no investment figure is cited here.

Why a casino-first phase is a common sequencing choice

Opening the gaming floor before the hotel is a well-established sequencing strategy in Indian Country. The casino generates cash flow and customer data while the more capital-intensive lodging and meeting space is completed, and it lets the operator tune staffing, marketing and table mix against real demand before the full resort is carrying fixed costs. We examined the logic and the tradeoffs of this approach in our look at phase-one market entry strategy.

The tradeoff is that a gaming-only property competes without one of the amenities that lengthens a visit and supports higher spend per trip. In a market with established regional competition, that gap can matter, which is why the 2027 hotel timeline will be watched as closely as the opening itself. The border location also invites direct comparison with commercial competition in nearby Illinois, a dynamic covered in our border-market analysis of Beloit and Rockford.

The delay itself is not unusual for a project of this scale. Late-stage commissioning of gaming systems, regulatory sign-offs, life-safety inspections and staffing are all common sources of schedule movement in the final weeks before a casino opens. Because the Nation did not state a cause, it would be speculative to attribute the shift to any one of them, and this article does not.

What the opening means for Wisconsin's tribal gaming landscape

Wisconsin is home to eleven federally recognized tribes, and tribal gaming there operates under Class III compacts with the state. Beloit is notable because it sits in the state's southern tier, far from the Nation's traditional gaming centers, and close to a major interstate corridor connecting Madison, Chicago and Rockford. For context on how Beloit fits the broader state picture, see our Wisconsin tribal gaming market deep dive.

For the Ho-Chunk Nation, the project also represents a diversification of its gaming footprint. Tribal governments reinvest casino revenue in health care, education, housing and other services, and a new property in a different market adds a revenue stream that is not tied to the Nation's existing locations. Readers tracking individual properties can find facility listings in the TribalGaming.com directory.

Several questions will define the first year. How quickly the 1,300-unit floor reaches its target performance, how the table and poker offering is received, how the Nation times and funds the 2027 hotel and convention center, and how competitors across the state line respond are all open. None can be answered from an opening announcement alone, and the Nation has not released projections that would let outside observers benchmark results.

For now, the practical information is simple: doors open to the public on Oct. 23, the casino runs 24 hours, and the lodging and meeting space that completes the resort is a fall 2027 deliverable. We will report on the hotel and convention center milestones as the Nation announces them.
